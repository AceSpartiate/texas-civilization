# What became of the people inside the Alamo — and what the game allows

Owner, 2026-09-22 (verbatim): *"Replace sex-only fictional fates with historically reviewed roles, location, choices, and
plausible escape/capture outcomes. Preserve smooth shared-world play and delayed news."* Astra's handoff adds: do not promise
universal rescue, do not change established historical individuals' fates, do not reveal remote events before news arrives,
and offer meaningful survival opportunities before they close. Survival and capture rules needed historical review before
they were built. This is that review, and the rules built from it.

Read with [MILITARY_EXPERIENCE.md](MILITARY_EXPERIENCE.md), [battle-research/alamo.md](battle-research/alamo.md) and the claim
registry in [HISTORY.md](../HISTORY.md) (`HIST-TEX-430` to `-439`, `FIC-GONZ-380` to `-386`).

## How this was researched

Checked 2026-09-22 through web research. The main sources were the TSHA *Handbook of Texas Online*, the Alamo's own site,
and Stephen L. Hardin's essay *Lines in the Soil; Lines on the Soul* (Hardin is the author of *Texian Iliad*, UT Press). The
essay was downloaded and read in full; the Handbook entries were read as pages. The printed books named below (Todish,
Lindley, Hansen, Davis, Crisp, Groneman) were **not** opened directly: they are cited through the Handbook and Hardin, and
where only Wikipedia or a secondary site carries a detail it is marked so. Treat this as a checked institutional reading,
not an archival study. Testimony is testimony; the disputes are attributed below.

## 1. What the record supports

### Everyone who lived had either left before the assault or was a noncombatant

- **Every fighting man still inside at dawn on 6 March was killed**, including the handful taken alive and then killed on
  Santa Anna's order (§3). Traditionally 189 defenders are named; research may raise it to about 257. The one documented
  exception among the fighters is Brigido Guerrero (§4). (TSHA *Alamo, Battle of the*; Hardin.) `HIST-TEX-435`
- **Running did not save anybody.** As many as seventy defenders went over the east and south walls during the assault;
  Ramírez y Sesma's lancers, posted there for that purpose, killed all but one, and that one was found and shot where he hid.
  (Hardin, citing Ramírez y Sesma's report as quoted in Davis, *Three Roads to the Alamo*, 1998.) `HIST-TEX-436`
- **About twenty women, children and enslaved people were spared** (§2). Being inside during the storming could still kill
  a noncombatant: a Black woman crossing the compound, a boy beside Enrique Esparza, and Anthony Wolf's two young sons are
  recorded as killed. "Spared" is the usual outcome, not a guaranteed one. (TSHA *Alamo Noncombatants*, Bill Groneman.)
  `HIST-TEX-433`

### The couriers (`HIST-TEX-431`)

Travis's riders went out at night, through or around the Mexican cavalry screen, relying on knowing the country:

| When | Who | What they carried | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 23 Feb | John W. Smith and Dr. John Sutherland (Sutherland's presence is questioned by some) | Word to the alcalde at Gonzales | thealamo.org *Albert Martin*; Wikipedia list citing Groneman, Lindley |
| 24 Feb | Albert Martin | Travis's letter "To the People of Texas & All Americans in the World"; handed on at Gonzales to Launcelot Smither | TSHA *Martin, Albert* |
| 25 Feb, about 9 p.m. | Juan N. Seguín with Antonio Cruz y Arocha | To raise help; met a Mexican patrol and got away by speaking Spanish and knowing the ground | TSHA *Seguín, Juan Nepomuceno*; TSHA *Cruz y Arocha, Antonio* |
| about 29 Feb | William S. Oury ("may have been sent") | — | TSHA *Oury, William Sanders* |
| 3 Mar, evening | John W. Smith | Travis's last dispatches, to the Convention and to Jesse Grimes (Jenkins, *Papers of the Texas Revolution* 4:502–505) | TSHA *Smith, John William*; Hardin |
| 5 Mar, evening | James L. Allen, the last courier | Messages toward Fannin at Goliad | TSHA *Allen, James L.* |

**No source read records a courier captured or killed while leaving the Alamo.** Two couriers went back in and died with the
garrison: Albert Martin with the Gonzales men on 1 March, and James Bonham, who rode in on 3 March. (TSHA *Martin*, *Bonham*.)
Some men were cut off outside and lived — Benjamin Highsmith, chased six miles by cavalry; Byrd Lockhart and Andrew Sowell,
out foraging. (TSHA *Highsmith*; Sons of DeWitt Colony *Alamo Couriers and Survivors*.) The lines tightened sharply on
3 March, when the last Mexican battalions arrived and a battery went up within musket shot of the north wall; from then on
only single riders at night got through. Nobody left on 6 March. `HIST-TEX-437`

### The noncombatants spared (`HIST-TEX-432`, `-433`)

- **Susanna Dickinson and her daughter Angelina**, of Gonzales; her husband Almeron, an artilleryman, was killed. She is the
  one documented family from outside Béxar. (TSHA *Dickinson, Susanna Wilkerson*.)
- **The Tejana women and children**: Ana Salazar de Esparza with her sons Enrique (about eight), Manuel and Francisco and
  her daughter; Juana Navarro Alsbury, her infant son and her sister Gertrudis Navarro; Concepción Losoya and her children;
  Victoriana de Salinas and her daughters; Petra Gonzales; Trinidad Saucedo. (TSHA *Alamo Noncombatants*.)
- **Afterwards**: the women and children were taken to Ramón Músquiz's house, questioned by Santa Anna, and each woman was
  given a blanket and two silver dollars. The Tejano families stayed in Béxar; Dickinson was sent east, escorted by Ben
  (Almonte's cook), with Joe, and was brought in to Houston at Gonzales. (TSHA *Dickinson*; TSHA *Tejanos and the Siege and
  Battle of the Alamo*.)
- **Joe**, enslaved by Travis, fought, took cover after Travis fell, was attacked when he came out and saved by a captain,
  was questioned and sent east with Dickinson, gave his account to the cabinet on 20 March — and was **returned to
  slavery**; he escaped on 21 April 1837. (TSHA *Joe*; the Alamo, [*Joe's Account*](https://www.thealamo.org/remember/battle-and-revolution/joes-account).)
  `HIST-TEX-434`. Bettie, a cook, and possibly Charlie are listed; Sam (Bowie's) is reported to have survived but nothing
  further is known (Wikipedia list) — **thin**.

### The Gonzales relief (`HIST-TEX-057`, `-438`)

Thirty-two men of Kimbell's Gonzales Ranging Company, with Albert Martin and guided by John W. Smith, got through the lines
about 3 a.m. on 1 March — the only relief that ever arrived. Every one of them died on 6 March. (TSHA *Alamo, Battle of the*;
TSHA *Kimbell, George C.*.)

### When the word came (`HIST-TEX-060`, `-439`)

Houston reached Gonzales on 11 March; within hours Andrés Barcenas and Anselmo Bergara brought word of the fall, and Houston
held them as spies. Dickinson arrived with Joe and Ben after dark about 12–13 March and confirmed it. Houston burned Gonzales
and the Runaway Scrape began. (TSHA *Dickinson*; Wikipedia *Battle of the Alamo*.)

## 2. What is disputed

- **Crockett's death.** Hardin finds "an overwhelming body of evidence" that Crockett was among the men executed after the
  battle; the Handbook calls it "almost impossible to determine how Crockett died"; Dickinson said he fell early, outside, and
  Joe that he was found among slain Mexicans. The de la Peña narrative, published 1955, is called a forgery by some; the
  Handbook notes it is a compiled narrative, not a day-by-day diary, and that de la Peña never claimed to see Crockett die.
  (TSHA *Crockett, David*; TSHA *Peña, José Enrique de la*; Crisp's introduction to *With Santa Anna in Texas*, Texas A&M
  Press, 1996, via Hardin.) **The executions themselves are well supported by several Mexican accounts; who was executed
  is not.** `HIST-TEX-435`
- **Louis "Moses" Rose**, who left about 3 March on foot, down the river and across the prairie. The "line in the sand"
  comes from W. P. Zuber's 1873 story; Zuber was not there and in 1877 admitted inventing part of Travis's speech. Davis:
  "the event simply did not happen"; Donovan (2012) defends it on second-hand evidence; some historians doubt Rose was there
  at all. (TSHA *Rose, Louis (Moses)*; Hardin.) `HIST-TEX-437`
- **Seguín's men during an alleged ceasefire**, Henry Warnell, and Madame Candelaria's claim to have been present — each
  disputed. (TSHA *Tejanos…*; Wikipedia list.)
- **What Bonham brought back** on 3 March: the Handbook says a letter from Robert M. Williamson promising help; the popular
  story says the news that Fannin was not coming.

## 3. Brigido Guerrero (`HIST-TEX-435`)

A deserter from the Mexican army who had joined Bowie's men; he hid, then convinced the soldiers he had been the Texians'
prisoner. Almonte's journal confirms a Mexican soldier held prisoner survived. He lived because he was Mexican and could pass
as a captive — a route no Anglo colonist had. (TSHA *Tejanos…*.) The game does not offer it.

## 4. What the game therefore allows

A student's family members are invented (`FIC-GONZ-001`, `-021`), but what can happen to them must be what the record
supports for somebody in that role and that place. **No established historical person's fate is changed**: the garrison
still falls on 6 March whatever a family does, Travis, Bowie, Crockett, Dickinson and Joe keep their registered outcomes, and
a family's person never takes a historical person's place.

**Role** (`alamoRole`, `FIC-GONZ-381`): a **fighter** is a man or a boy of sixteen and up — an age to answer for the family,
the same line as everywhere else in the game — however he came to be inside, sick or well (Bowie was killed in his bed). A
**noncombatant** is every woman and every younger child.

Smith guided the relief in and rode out with Travis's last letters on March 3. | `HIST-TEX-057`, `-438`, `-431` |

**Survival opportunities, offered in words before they close** (`FIC-GONZ-382`, `-383`):

1. **Before the siege**: the garrison can be sent for. When the rumour comes (about 18 February) every family with somebody in
   the garrison is told they can still be sent for, and that once the Mexican army is in the town nobody can be. The card's
   recall button says the same.
2. **During the siege**: on each of the four nights Travis sent riders out (24 and 25 February, 3 and 5 March), his runner
   walks to every played fighter inside and says so; the man can offer. About one offer in four is chosen that night
   (`COURIER_CHOSEN`, `FIC-GONZ-045`). A volunteer passed over, and a man who stayed, are **asked again** on each later night
   (owner, 2026-09-22). On 3 March the runner says the lines are closer every night; on 5 March that there may not be many
   more chances. Nothing says when, or whether, the assault will come.

**Not offered, and why**:

- **Leaving over the wall, or alone during the siege.** The one case (Rose) is disputed and the men who ran at the assault
  were cut down. An escape button that the record says fails would be a trap, and one that succeeds would be invented.
- **Surrender, capture or hiding as a fighter.** The men taken alive were killed; Guerrero's route was not a colonist's.
- **A noncombatant killed.** The record includes children and a woman killed inside. The game spares every woman and child
  inside; this is a deliberate simplification (`ceiling:` in the owner's hands — see HANDOFF's questions), not a claim that
  nobody else died.
- **A noncombatant sent out.** No woman or child is recorded carrying Travis's letters.
- **Enslaved people in a family.** The families in this game are not slaveholders and no family member is enslaved; Joe's
  story is his own and is not a mechanic (`HIST-TEX-434`; MILITARY_EXPERIENCE.md "Survivors and Joe").

**No fate before the word** (`HIST-TEX-439`): the world knows what happened at dawn on 6 March; a family's screen does not
until the word reaches it — at Gonzales the rumour on the 11th and Dickinson's confirmation on the 13th, elsewhere that
evening. A spared woman or child walking home is the word, arriving in person.

**Nobody answered** (`FIC-GONZ-384`, `-385`): if a student does not answer Travis's runner within the question's real-time
budget (90 seconds by default, not counted while the Host has paused), or before the riders go that night, the person decides
alone at auto's share — the owner's standing rule that auto takes over a choice not made in time (`FIC-GONZ-048`) — and the
journal says so in plain words. The review would also defend **staying at one's post** as the fallback (Travis chose his
riders from men who offered); which of the two is the owner's to choose.

**Deterministic** (`FIC-GONZ-008`, `-045`): who offers on auto, who is chosen, and each person's place inside the walls come
from `share(world, personId, question)` — the same for the same class every time, nothing hidden and re-rolled.
