# Military service as a lived experience

Owner direction, 2026-09-21: slow military service and battles substantially, skip selected quiet intervals, alert the player through people, and let them attend and influence a family member's experience. This direction supersedes earlier interface notes that forbid invitations or discussion of danger. It does not authorize invented historical certainty. Read with [VISION](../VISION.md), [living information](LIVING_INFORMATION.md), and [the Alamo research](battle-research/alamo.md).

## What this pass actually implements

- `sim/military-pacing.mjs`: attended military travel has a **120-calendar-minute maximum per live tick**, versus the campaign's previous 720. Open Alamo courier, army, and Houston questions cap a tick at **20 minutes**. Only living, uncaptured people of played, present households, not on auto, request this extra time. A selected courier or recalled volunteer keeps journey protection on the way out/home after release from service. Stationary camp without a decision remains compressible. These values are gameplay tuning, not historical measurements.
- Army camp relocation now starts a visible journey without consuming a free interval on the order tick; a stationary army at a named camp remains quiet time.
- Live ticks land on existing historical barriers while such people are involved. A twelve-hour tick must not open and close a short military opportunity in one update. The existing director still owns dates and consequences.
- `withCalendarStep` in `sim/clock.mjs` keeps one interval throughout `stepWorld`: travel cannot re-read a faster calendar after the tick has already landed on a boundary. A transient WeakMap is cleared even after an exception; no clock state enters saves.
- `sim/time.mjs`: explicit time jumps refuse to pass an attended military journey or unanswered military decision. Existing contact and historical barriers remain. No new automatic jump button was added.
- `public/military-attention.js` and the message card: a visible, nonblocking invitation for an actual encountered rider, an Alamo courier request, an army/Houston question, or the family's besieged person. A button selects/follows that person and opens the existing decision. It does not volunteer, move them, or advance the story. New requests reopen the card; it can be folded, and multiple requests can be cycled. Seen invitations are remembered in session storage, scoped to class and household; they are not knowledge or authoritative acknowledgements.
- An actual rider's invitation reveals no report contents. A request inside the fort is explicitly **at that person's side**, not a claim that a rider reached their distant family. This pass does **not** add a new physical Alamo messenger actor. That work is first in the continuation below.
- Removed the inaccurate blanket claim that no man inside the Alamo survived from two player/Host messages. The legacy fictional-player fate calculation is unchanged and still needs a role-based replacement.

At Study pace (9.5 real seconds per tick), a ten-hour courier decision now spans approximately 30 ticks / 4 minutes 45 seconds, rather than the prior news pace's ten ticks / 95 seconds. Quick pace still shortens real reading time; absolute real-time minimums are not implemented. A campaign travel interval formerly taking one tick now takes six. Quiet camp remains faster. These are calculations, not classroom playtest results.

## Current limits — do not describe this as a finished battle system

The Alamo assault still resolves through `stormAlamo` in one simulation update. This pass makes existing decisions more discoverable and gives them time; it does not provide playable room-by-room combat, staged casualties, new rescue actions, or a rendered courier departure scene. The player can follow their person and answer the existing courier offer. Animation alone would not fix the missing encounter structure.

Other remaining limitations: some army transitions set a position directly; `reliefEnters` snaps arriving relief to Béxar. Courier departure catches an unreachable route after declaring release. The four courier dates currently ask each person only once; a rejected volunteer is not reconsidered. Courtly phrasing and a chance roll are not sufficient agency for the final game. Outcome projection still uses delayed household knowledge, and some Alamo text/state can remain a last-known siege view after the actual fall. The existing sex-based fictional outcome rule is a prototype simplification, not a valid account of everyone inside the compound. Preserve old saves while replacing these deliberately.

The new global pacing affects every household sharing that tick, including civilian work and consumption. The existing effort/calendar split is unchanged, so more real time also gives more effort ticks within a historical day. Rebalance only after measuring that effect, not by silently restoring fast military ticks. No claim is made that the full campaign still fits one 45-minute period.

## Experience to build next

**Enlistment and attachment.** Explain the destination, expected absence, what the household loses by sending this person, and the commitments they accept. Show a farewell, equipment preparation and departure using that person's stable identity. Give refusal, supply support and civilian protection equal legitimacy. Arrive at a camp through its road/entrance, meet a named comrade, and take an initial duty. Do not replace a departing person with a generic soldier token who later becomes them again.

**Journey, camp, danger, aftermath.** These are distinct pacing modes. A journey has visible departure, selected waypoints, a rest and arrival. Routine miles and uneventful camp periods may compress, but approaching contact, a changed order, a crossing, an injury, a separation or a departure deadline ends compression before the consequence. Keep some quiet intervals for letters, recovery and family decisions; a montage of disasters cannot create attachment. Never accelerate a figure's walk cycle to represent days passing. Preserve continuous local action, camera position, input, sound and travel progress. Do not use a dated transition, blackout, montage, loading card or automatic viewpoint switch to conceal compression. The seamless-continuity contract below supersedes the earlier proposal for a dated transition.

**A person brings the interruption.** At home, reuse the actual dispatch/route/encounter pipeline. In camp or the Alamo, create a persistent runner or courier who walks/rides from the command post to the serving person. The invitation says who is being approached; opening it frames both people. The first spoken statement establishes what was heard. Deliver distant news only when a carrier actually arrives. An offscreen family member remains a playable viewpoint, but being allowed to watch them must not automatically teach the homestead everything they see. Queue concurrent requests, permit defer/reopen, and never steal a camera during a drag or a command.

**The Alamo.** The whole scaled compound, traversable interiors and destructible north-wall assets already have their own art/layout contracts. Bind the persistent person to that coordinate system; do not resize the fort around a cinematic. Stage arrival, siege duties, courier opportunities, preparations, alarm, the assault's successive locations, and aftermath. Each stage needs readable movement, cover, meaningful work and persistent consequences. Scene markers must describe what is happening locally, not disclose an exact future attack time to players who cannot know it.

**Agency with honest limits.** Before the final assault, plausible opportunities include serving as a selected courier, leaving on an authorized errand, or declining entry before the relief departs. During the siege: carrying ammunition/water, aiding a wounded person, relieving an exhausted sentry, supporting a courier, and sheltering civilians. Implement one complete chain first; do not add six buttons backed only by flavor text. Every action needs an observable effect and a cost. State uncertainty honestly. Do not offer an escape button that secretly cannot succeed, or promise survival if the historical mode disallows it. The fort's fall remains fixed in historical mode, while the family's presence and earlier choices may change. Any later counterfactual survival mode must be explicitly labeled and separately tested.

**Survivors and Joe.** Avoid “all men died” and “only women survived.” Joe, enslaved by William Barret Travis, survived after participating in the defense; his account describes returning to quarters after Travis fell. His experience must be authored from reviewed sources, with his humanity and constrained choices intact. It is not a generic disguise mechanic or a racial survival bonus. Roles, individual circumstances and evidence—not a sex switch—must drive named historical cases. The family's fictional defenders must not displace Joe or overwrite his recorded testimony. See [the Alamo's account](https://www.thealamo.org/remember/battle-and-revolution/joes-account) and [its early reports](https://www.thealamo.org/remember/battle-and-revolution/early-reports), checked 2026-09-21. Treat testimony as testimony; disputed details need attribution.

**Loss and survival.** Do not cut straight from a moving sprite to a death entry in the journal. Resolve a present person's final sequence, separate actual fate from distant knowledge, carry bodies/wounds/capture forward, and let survivors tell what they witnessed. A delayed messenger must not retroactively change the underlying event. Return to the family with a letter, belongings, uncertainty or a survivor, then give them space and choices. Do not reward casualty avoidance by erasing the significance of Goliad or the Alamo, and do not require a household tragedy as a teaching quota.

## Seamless-continuity contract — owner clarification, 2026-09-22

The player should experience one uninterrupted world. Pacing modes are internal scheduling decisions, not visible game modes. “Smooth” includes responsiveness and continuity of cause and effect, not just interpolated sprite positions.

- **Local motion has a stable rhythm.** Locomotion, gait, recoil, reloading, projectiles, injury reactions, dialogue and interaction progress use bounded, consistent local elapsed time. A change to calendar compression must not multiply animation or visible movement speed. Keep the camera attached to the same authoritative person and preserve cursor, selection and held inputs. No wall, doorway, ford or final approach is crossed between two visible samples without traversing its path.
- **Do not smooth a teleport cosmetically.** Interpolation must stay within the authoritative traversable route and a small measured display-latency budget. Never leave an animated person walking toward a battle that the server has already killed them in. Bound the server advance before presentation falls behind; freeze neither input nor a displayed actor while hidden simulation resolves their next scene.
- **Compress only genuinely unobserved routine.** Calendar savings may accumulate over quiet, noninteractive intervals. A followed traveller, a visible local encounter, an unresolved choice, a player placing an order, or a threatened family member is a protected continuity interval. If no safe compression interval exists, let the session run longer. Do not force a cut, move the camera away, spawn a distraction, or hide a visible person merely to satisfy a fixed lesson runtime.
- **Enter and leave quiet time before the seam reaches the player.** Look ahead to the earliest meaningful arrival or danger. Reduce world advance while there is still safe headroom, so the approaching rider, marching column and changing weather enter the playable area at normal local speed. Position and velocity must remain continuous; do not simply blend the calendar rate while leaving distances to jump. The current hard tick caps are a first safeguard, not proof that this scheduler exists.
- **Keep the environment continuous too.** Daylight, shadows, weather, ambient sound, smoke and crowds must not snap when scheduling changes. Respect the same authoritative date and weather; do not render a false sunny afternoon during a mechanically decisive night attack. If a coherent environmental transition cannot fit, defer compression. Avoid a rapidly spinning date display or a “time skipped” banner in play; precise dates and elapsed intervals remain available in the journal.
- **Agency never disappears in a transition.** A player can keep moving, inspect their family and respond. The scheduler yields before processing a new command or consequential contact. An existing conversation remains open with the same speaker and line, and its decision budget is protected. UI notification collapse is only presentation, never permission to advance past the question.
- **One shared world.** No client gets a private timeline, duplicated battle or retroactive rescue. The server arbitrates protected intervals across households. Camera/engagement signals may request presentation protection only after validation; they grant no visibility or knowledge and cannot be used to reveal hidden actors. Bound abuse and absent-player holds, but never resolve a present player's protected scene solely to meet a period-length target.
- **Reconnect is continuity, not a replay reward.** Resume the persistent actor, route, scene phase and unanswered choice. Reconstruct only already-known context. Do not replay the departure, grant another survival roll, or turn a stale last-known position into a live view.

Acceptance must include recorded uninterrupted play across camp-to-march, distant-to-local arrival, courier approach, opening/closing a decision, battle alarm and aftermath, at all paces and with network jitter. Measure position/velocity discontinuities, maximum display lag, input latency and environmental changes across seams. Prove each boundary with keyboard and touch while another household is busy. In unprompted playtests, players should be able to explain what their person did and why without describing a fast-forward, a teleport or a cutscene interruption. Do not advertise seamless play until both measurements and observation support it.


## Architecture and bounded implementation order for Claude

1. **One complete local Alamo courier encounter.** Add a server-owned scene/encounter record with stable IDs for scene, phase, person, messenger, location, opened tick/minute, deadline, choices, acknowledgement and resulting command IDs. Reuse existing encounter conventions; do not send all future dialogue branches to the client. Stage the runner approaching, conversation, voluntary answer, selection result and physical departure. Save/reload at each point. Replace the present text-only local invitation only after this chain works.
2. **Battle phases before new tactical breadth.** Implement an encounter scheduler for `alarm -> approach -> contact -> fallback -> aftermath`. The actual phases and durations must be researched per engagement. Persist phase entry and resolved effects. The same person, army, inventory, injury and room state continue across phases and reconnects. Start with one playable Alamo duty/courier chain and one observed assault sequence; then reuse the architecture for Gonzales, Concepción, Béxar, Goliad and San Jacinto.
3. **Actor/location authority.** Move into rooms through doors, use line of sight and roof cutaways, keep collisions and north-wall breach state authoritative, and validate every interaction's range. Never fast-forward someone through a wall. Interrupted courier routes resume at the same point; blocked routes produce a visible alternative or refusal, not a success message followed by standing still.
4. **Protected decision budgets.** Target 60–120 real seconds for an unfamiliar consequential question and 3–6 real minutes for a substantial attended combat scene; tune through playtests, not historical-time claims. Reconcile shared-world calendar and wall-clock budgets centrally. Cap extension for absent/auto households. A player cannot freeze the entire class forever by leaving a popup open, and host-selected Quick cannot silently eliminate the last chance to answer. Warn visibly before an unanswered choice resolves, without a false precision countdown about unknowable history.
5. **Selective quiet-time scheduler.** Compute the next permissible boundary from all households: meaningful contact, travel waypoint, arrival, battle phase, choice deadline, injury/capture, evacuation danger and any pending interactive task. Compression replays authoritative small steps, records a causal summary and cannot pass the earliest protected event. Preserve pending/declined/missed messages and work state. Put a modest minimum quiet interval between attention demands; stagger noncritical messages rather than queuing several modal dialogs.
6. **A real family debrief.** Track who witnessed a loss, what relatives have actually heard, unfinished promises, wounded survivors, carried letters and returned possessions. Present this through people; the journal is a memory afterward. Bring the same histories into later service and evacuation choices.

## Completion gates

- Simulate 5, 15 and 30 households with mixed service, civilian, absent and auto participation. Record real-time decision windows at every pace, the longest unattended period, travel pop distances, missed requests, civilian effort gained per calendar day, and total period length.
- A request opens on one update and cannot close on that same update. Live progression and explicit compression stop at the same protected boundaries. An unanswered request eventually resolves under a documented policy. Nobody can extend it indefinitely by reconnecting.
- A distant household receives neither alert payload nor sealed fate before real contact. Only the participant viewpoint has local knowledge. Test Host, owner, another household, spectators and reconnect snapshots for leakage.
- One courier scene and one battle sequence can be played entirely with actual UI input on desktop and a 390px phone, not just forced states. Include keyboard, reduced motion, simultaneous messages, refresh during a choice, and reconnect after dispatch.
- A choice demonstrably changes persistent state beyond dialogue; repeating the command does not repeat its effects. Test carry/support success, interruption, route failure and a valid refusal. Named historical people retain their registered outcomes.
- Draw the person walking every used entrance and room at the compound's established scale. Test a destroyed wall before and after reload; test camera focus, occlusion and route replanning without teleportation.
- Review all final lines and casualty rules against the claim registry. Clearly label reconstructed actions and disputed testimony. Do not call a battle release-ready on the strength of screenshots alone.

## Evidence for this first pass

`tests/military-pacing.test.mjs` and `tests/military-attention.test.mjs` cover pacing, boundary handling, the transient tick clock, time-jump blocking, message privacy and request ordering. The three initial pacing tests were observed failing against the original implementation; a separate regression reproduced accelerated departure after release before that was repaired. `node scripts/military-regression-check.mjs` also detects seven isolated mutations (privacy, report leakage, queue priority, army requests, a changing tick clock, bypassed jump guards and a camp misread as marching); evidence is in `docs/evidence/military-injections.json`. The existing Alamo/browser proof is expanded to enter the real creation flow, open the siege invitation, find the courier request, send its real answer, and check phone bounds. Validation: full suite 930 passed, then 35 focused military tests passed after the final released-courier fix; Alamo browser 6 checks and tutorial browser 21 checks passed, without page errors. Logs are `test-results/military-full-suite.log`, `military-final-focused.log`, `military-browser.log`, and `military-lesson-browser.log`. `HANDOFF.md` records the scope and limits. The rest of this document is a continuation plan, not implemented functionality.

**Finished 2026-09-22.** The injection harness was rebuilt: each mutation is still applied by a load hook in a child process, but the harness now runs the **whole** test file, requires exactly the named test to fail and no other, runs a clean baseline first, and throws when a mutation target is not found exactly once (the earlier version ran only the selected test, so it could not see a mutation that broke a neighbour too). It covers **13** mutations, one per test and two on the camp order: the seven above plus an auto person slowing the class, an absent family slowing it, a boundary stepped over, a released courier sped up, and a camp order read as an arrival or given a free tick of marching. That last pair guards a new test (`a camp order starts a visible journey`); before it, nothing failed if `moveCamp` stopped clearing the camp. **13 of 13 caught**, `docs/evidence/military-injections.json`. The pacing claims are registered as `FIC-GONZ-320` to `-322` in [HISTORY.md](../HISTORY.md) (an earlier draft used `FIC-GONZ-232`, inside the travel-sight block). The deliberate simplifications carry `ceiling:` comments in `sim/military-pacing.mjs`, `sim/time.mjs` and `public/military-attention.js`. Steps 1–6 above remain unbuilt; they need the owner's go-ahead before they displace the Gonzales/core usability phase.
