# Béxar civic façades: art research and limits

## San Fernando, before the Gothic enlargement

The [Library of Congress/NPS HABS TX-34 report](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/pnp/habshaer/tx/tx0000/tx0012/data/tx0012data.pdf), PDF pp. 8–9 (printed addendum pp. 4–5), describes the earlier church from nineteenth-century views: rubble limestone with stucco, plain rectangular front, round-arched doorway, stepped side buttresses, parapet over vaults, polygonal transepts/apse and a low crossing dome. It identifies one visible southern octagonal bell cupola with arched openings and a pyramidal cap. The northern tower may never have been built. The old front and tower were demolished for the later work.

**Art inference:** use that earlier massing to depict 1836, omitting the modern twin Gothic towers and triple portals. The described views postdate the 1841 repairs, so they do not prove the exact 1836 plaster condition, cap finish, ornament, opening dimensions or roof condition. These are subdued illustrative choices, not a measured elevation. The sprite shows one bell cupola and the separate low crossing-dome lantern; the latter is not a second bell tower. No flags or people are baked into the building.

The [Texas State Historical Association history](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/san-fernando-cathedral) corroborates the later Gothic rebuilding beginning in 1868 and the earlier church's role during the Revolution. Treat its disputed burial account separately from this exterior delivery.

## Governor's Palace / Comandancia / Pérez residence

The [City of San Antonio's site history](https://www.sanantonio.gov/governorspalace/History-Culture) records the six-room property in 1804, Pérez-family residence thereafter, and an enlarged, romanticized 1930 restoration. Therefore today's landscaped museum is not a literal 1836 reference.

The [TSHA Comandancia account](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/spanish-governors-palace-comandancia) supports substantial masonry/plaster walls, wooden lintels, roof drainage spouts and the carved entry stone. It describes courtyard expansion before 1804, but the present restored site cannot independently establish every 1836 opening or courtyard feature. This delivery depicts the front only. The THC nomination download timed out and SAH full page returned 403; neither was used as a verified elevation reference.

**Art inference:** a restrained, long, one-storey plastered masonry front with flat parapeted terrado roof, recessed wooden entrance, small stylized heraldic relief, window grilles and canales. Opening number/spacing, weathering and the short visible side are illustrative. No legible historical inscription is invented. No modern garden, fountain, pergola or speculative courtyard wings are shown.

## Game scale and scope

These are exterior scenery sprites matching existing elevated painted façades. Existing placement IDs, coordinates and stylized heightFeet remain; these are not archaeological footprint measurements. Compare the doorway with the production character rendering, not an assumed physical ruler. Roof separation, interior rooms, navigation and destruction are outside this explicitly static façade request and must be designed before any cutaway use.
