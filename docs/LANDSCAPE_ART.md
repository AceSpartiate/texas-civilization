# Waterways, roads and crossings

The shared `public/landscape-art.js` renderer dresses the existing map polylines with layered water depth, bank margins, surface highlights, bank stones, road shoulders and paired wheel ruts. Visible-segment sampling has a bounded detail budget; off-screen river miles do not exhaust that budget. The renderer does not smooth a river into a different course or change road travel distances.

Fords use shallow water, exposed stones and small ripples instead of the former horizontal dashed marker. They align perpendicular to the nearest projected watercourse. A separate timber-deck treatment supports an explicitly projected `bridge` site. **No bridge site was added to the simulation:** existing fords remain fords. Exact bridge locations and historical construction still require source-backed scenario data. This is illustrative construction, not an engineering design.

The same road and river treatments are shared by the main world and the Béxar/Gonzales scenic assemblies. Widths retain the current map's scale rules. Materials are canvas artwork, not edited raster assets. No image-generation prompt or invented raster provenance applies to these code-native surfaces. Water highlights are currently static; moving water and more detailed researched bridge assets remain further art work.

Checks: real-map Gonzales browser rendering and phone width pass. Existing simulation tests cover river barriers, allowed crossings, routing and saved geography; those data are unchanged. The standalone visual comparison can be generated by `scripts/landscape-art-proof.mjs` to inspect water, dirt road, ford and explicit bridge treatments together.

## Painted road tiles (2026-10-13)

Owner, 2026-10-13: *"use our new image generation setup to improve roads."* The roads, the town streets and squares, and a
family's paths and yard are now laid in four painted tiles - the first art in the library from the owner's local pipeline
(ComfyUI + FLUX.2 klein: painted with klein 9B on 2026-10-13, [ART_DELIVERY_2026-10-13-ROAD-TILES.md](ART_DELIVERY_2026-10-13-ROAD-TILES.md), and
repainted with klein 4B on 2026-10-26, whose licence allows the game to be sold, [ART_DELIVERY_2026-10-26-ROAD-TILES-4B.md](ART_DELIVERY_2026-10-26-ROAD-TILES-4B.md)). So the
paragraph above is no longer the whole truth: the verge, the dirt, the packed street and the trodden path are a raster now,
with recorded prompts and provenance; the geometry they are laid along, the widths and the crossings are unchanged.

`public/ground-texture.js` lays them. The street and the verge are **flat** tiles, a pattern pinned to the world's origin so
the ground stays put as the map is dragged; the road and the path are tiles **laid along** the way, turned to each piece of it
and carried on from piece to piece, so the painted ruts run along the road round its bends. A road laid in the tiles drops the
drawn ruts and grit (the tile has its own); its grass tufts are kept. Each tile is kept at 512 down to 32 pixels, made once.
While the `ground-roads` sheet has not arrived, every way is drawn in its old flat strokes, exactly as before.

`ceiling:` a tile laid along a way is turned per straight chord of the curve, so on a tight bend at close zoom the ruts kink at
each point rather than curving; seen only at a sharp bend close in (docs/evidence/roads-after-*.png). Turning the tile per
step along the drawn curve (`curvePoints`) is the way out if it is ever noticed.
