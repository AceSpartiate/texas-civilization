## The one-line answer

**After Gonzales burned on the night of March 13, Houston fell back east with the army and the families went with it or ahead
of it: the Runaway Scrape.** Washington was empty by March 17, the Brazos settlements and Richmond about April 1, Nacogdoches
and San Augustine a little before April 13. The flight was made "by any means of transportation or none at all", through
cold, rain and hunger, and "many persons died and were buried where they fell"; no count of them exists. Houston crossed the
Colorado on March 17 and had about 1,200 men near Columbus by March 20. When word of Fannin's defeat came on March 25, many
men left the army to see to their families. He was at San Felipe by March 28 and at Groce's on the Brazos from March 30,
where he drilled the army for a fortnight. Santa Anna crossed the Brazos at Richmond on April 11 with about 700 men, reached
Harrisburg on the 15th, and went after the government. Houston turned toward him and met him at San Jacinto. **At San
Jacinto on the afternoon of April 21, 910 Texans attacked about 1,200 Mexicans; it "lasted but eighteen minutes"; nine
Texans were killed or mortally wounded and thirty wounded; 630 Mexicans were killed and 730 taken prisoner. Santa Anna was
found and captured the next day.** Then the refugees turned home, "many toward homes that no longer existed".

**Meanwhile Goliad.** Fannin was ordered to fall back to Victoria about March 12–14, but first sent King (about 30 men) and
Ward's Georgia Battalion (about 120) to Refugio. King's men were captured and executed March 16; Ward's men escaped and were
taken near Dimitt's Landing March 22 (about 85). Fannin left Goliad in fog on March 19, was caught on the open prairie at
Coleto Creek with ten killed and sixty or more wounded, and surrendered about 330 men on March 20; Horton and about 30
mounted men got away. **On Palm Sunday, March 27, 342 prisoners were shot on the roads outside Goliad, and Fannin and about
forty wounded were killed inside the presidio; 28 escaped; about 20 were spared as physicians, orderlies, interpreters or
mechanics**, many at the pleading of Francita Alavez, "the Angel of Goliad".
