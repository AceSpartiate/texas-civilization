## The one-line answer

**For three and a half weeks after November 2 the army sat outside Béxar and did not attack. It split into a camp at
the old mill half a mile to a mile north of the town and a camp at Mission Concepción two miles south, and united at
the mill on November 15. It sent parties out after horses, convoys and Ugartechea, burned the grass toward the Rio
Grande, ran out of flour and corn, and lost men going home while others arrived. On November 21 Austin ordered an
assault and could not find a hundred men willing. On November 24, 405 men pledged to stay and elected Edward
Burleson; Austin left the next day. On November 26 Deaf Smith reported a Mexican pack train coming in from the west.
Bowie with about forty horsemen and William H. Jack with about a hundred infantry drawn from many companies caught it
in the dry creek beds by the Alazán, about a mile west of the town. Cos sent out infantry and a cannon, and they were
driven back into town. The packs held cut grass, not silver.** No Texian was killed. Jack counted three slightly
wounded and one missing among his infantry; the usual total is **four wounded and one missing (he ran home)**. Mexican
losses are disputed, **from 3 killed and 14 wounded (Barr) to "sixty" killed (Bowie)**. About 140 to 300 Texians were
engaged, so the rates the sources support are **0% killed and about 1.3–2.9% wounded** (computed here).

## 2. Summary

- **November 2.** Both councils of war vote not to storm. Bowie's division below the town votes to join the main army
  above it. Burleson holds the mill.
- **November 3–7.** "More than 150 men" have gone home for winter clothing (Austin, Nov 4); the effective force is
  "about 450 men after deducting the sick". Three cannon and "reenforcements of 180 men" arrive (Nov 5). East Texas
  companies elect a major and a lieutenant colonel (Nov 7). Bryan counts "near 800 men in camp" (Nov 7).
- **November 8–15.** Austin moves headquarters back south to Mission Concepción; Burleson commands the upper
  division at "the mill station". A scout, Mr. House, is killed by a fall from his horse on the 8th and the party
  bringing in his body fights off Mexican cavalry. Travis takes about 300 poor Mexican horses (reported Nov 11).
  Fannin rides down the Laredo road after a rumoured convoy (Nov 9–17). Ugartechea slips out of Béxar with 100–150
  cavalry (night of Nov 13 or 14). Burleson's division threatens to go home unless the army is united; it is united at
  the mill on the 15th, and Burleson rides after Ugartechea.
- **November 16–22.** Orders against shooting, gambling and killing beef; horses tied up at night; corn rationed. Bryan
  counts "about 6 hundred men in camp" (Nov 18). Austin orders a storm for dawn on the 22nd; both division commanders
  report their men unwilling, and "not more than 100 men" can be found. A battery is begun within 300 yards of the
  walls. Morris's company of New Orleans Greys joins on the afternoon of the 21st. "The army is now out of Flour and
  the corn is exhausted" (Nov 22).
- **November 24–25.** The army is paraded; **405 men** pledge to stay; **Burleson is elected unanimously**. Austin
  leaves on the 25th.
- **November 26. The Grass Fight** (§4).
- **November 27 – December 4.** Burleson's report reaches the General Council at San Felipe by December 1. Maverick and
  others are let out of Béxar on December 1 and urge an attack. The army nearly breaks up and talks of winter quarters.
  On December 4 Milam calls for volunteers; more than 300 answer. The storming begins at dawn on December 5 (§9).

**Reading:** the army in camp ran between about 400 and 800, most often about 600, with men going and coming every
week. The documented counts are Austin's and Bryan's estimates plus one parade (405).

### 3.3 Who came and who went

- **Came:** 180 men with three cannon (Nov 5). The Nacogdoches and East Texas companies under Rusk (TSHA, "reinforcements
  from East Texas led by Thomas J. Rusk"), who elected their own field officers on Nov 7; Sublett, of San Augustine,
  commanded one of the two divisions by Nov 21. Garner's nineteen (Nov 16). **The New Orleans Greys:** Morris's company
  joined on the afternoon of Nov 21 (Order Book); **Barr puts the Greys' arrival on November 18 (via Wikipedia)**, and
  TSHA speaks of "three companies with over a hundred men" from the United States in mid-November. Breece's company came
  overland by Nacogdoches and Bastrop (TSHA, New Orleans Greys; Ehrenberg) on a date not found. Cooke later reported 70
  men in his (formerly Morris's) company and 50 in Breece's. Deserters from Béxar came in (Nov 14–16).
- **Went:** "more than 150" for winter clothing by Nov 4, all having "promised to return". "Desertion more or less every
  day" until about Nov 18 (Bryan). Rolls on Nov 21 were to list "those who have left yesterday or today". Smithwick (not
  there) gives the joke of the day: a man leaving said he "was going after that cannon". Officers resigned or offered to: Travis
  (Nov 6), Wharton (Nov 8), Adjutant General Hall (Nov 18). Austin (Nov 25).
- **What held men:** the Nov 10 order that "no one leave the camp without written permission"; the Nov 24 pledge and
  roll; and, the other way, the Nov 12 threat that "whole companies would leave and go home" unless the divisions were
  united, which Austin gave in to. **No man in the sources read was punished for going home.**

### 3.4 Supply, weather, sickness, discipline

- **Food.** Beef and corn from the country, corn gathered from the Béxar fields and rationed (Nov 16–17); "no one kill a
  beef" without the quartermaster. **By Nov 22: "out of Flour and the corn is exhausted."** On Nov 26 Wharton writes
  that the army lacks "coffee, sugar, flour, tobacco, clothing". Mexicans were forbidden to take anything into town
  (Nov 6, 10, 16).
- **Clothing.** Blankets, shoes and tent cloth were on the road from San Felipe (Oct 31). Bryan asks home for "4 or 5 prs
  of socks", "1 cloth round jacket", pants for his brother and "my pot metal boots as I will soon be barefooted" (Nov 7).
- **Guns.** Three cannon arrived Nov 5. The heavy battering gun was lost at sea and landed on Bird Island (Hall, Nov 23).
  Men picked up Mexican six-pound shot to fire back: "about twenty" (Austin, Nov 18); a "small breach in the walls of the
  church" (Telegraph Nov 21); "no balls to do much good" (Austin, Nov 22).
- **Weather.** "A cold and stormy night" at San Felipe, Nov 4 (Borden). "Excessively bad" weather and "very muddy"
  roads on the coast, Nov 18–23 (Hall). The creek forded on Nov 26 was "cold wide and deep" (Jack). Austin's report of
  Nov 30 calls it "the most inclement, wet, and cold spell of weather known in this country for many years" (Telegraph
  Dec 12). **No day-by-day weather record was found.** "Norther" does not appear in the sources read.
- **Sickness.** Austin's "severe dysentery" from the Cibolo to about Nov 15. The Nov 4 effective count deducts "the
  sick". "No medicine in camp" and no surgical supplies (Nov 4). **No death from sickness in camp between Nov 2 and
  Dec 4 was found.** Captain James Burleson, 60, "became ill after being discharged" and died at Bastrop on January 3,
  1836 (TSHA, James Burleson Sr.), which is after the window.
- **Deaths in the window.** Mr. House, by a fall from his horse, Nov 8 (Telegraph Nov 21). No Texian killed by the enemy
  between Nov 2 and Dec 4 in any source read.
- **Discipline.** Orders against firing guns in camp (Nov 12, 16), gambling and noise (Nov 16), leaving without written
  leave (Nov 10), sleeping on sentry (Nov 10), private use of captured horses (Nov 10). Alcohol: "no whysky" (Nov 4–5).
  Wharton resigned because orders were not enforced (Nov 8). The councils refused to storm three times (Nov 2, Nov 21;
  Austin: "at various times"). **The army voted on its own commander and on whether to stay.**

## 4. The Grass Fight, 26 November 1835

### 4.1 The date

**November 26 is DOCUMENTED.** Jack's report of November 27 says "on Yesterday"; the Council resolution says "the
26th day of November"; the Telegraph (Dec 2) says "the 26th ult."; TSHA says the 26th. Creed Taylor's "November 27, I
think" and the "28th" that Brown says one account gives are wrong. **Kennedy (1841) calls the November 8 skirmish "the
Grass Fight"**; Yoakum says that is a confusion.

### 4.2 The ground

- **Jack (primary):** the infantry forded "the creek which though cold wide and deep" at a trot; about half an hour later,
  "in tolerably good order and in double file", they were fired on at "forty to sixty yards" by an enemy "concealed in a
  ditch and completely hidden by the thick muskeet [mesquite] bushes". His two divisions flanked right and left and
  "the ditch and field were cleared". The enemy fell back "under the protection of their batteries in town".
- **Yoakum:** the foraging party was on "the old Presidio road"; Bowie met it "about a mile from the town"; the Mexicans
  "took a position in the bed of a dry branch"; afterward the Texans "formed in a ravine".
- **Barr (TSHA):** "near Alazán Creek west of town"; the two sides "soon fought on foot from ravines". Via Wikipedia: about
  a mile from Béxar, where the Mexicans were crossing a dry ravine.
- **Groneman (via Wikipedia) and the marker:** near the junction of the Alazán, Apache and San Pedro creeks.
- **Taylor:** "On the Alazan Creek, a short distance from town, at the point where the Castroville Road now crosses."
- **Coordinates (the marker, not the battlefield):** **29.4226 N, 98.5138 W**, at W. César E. Chávez Blvd and S. Brazos
  St., San Antonio (marker transcriptions; the street description varies between them, the coordinates agree). **Computed
  here:** that is about **1.2 miles west and 0.1 mile south** of the repository's Béxar point, which fits "about a mile
  from the town". In the units of `MISSIONS` in `sim/army.mjs` (miles east and south of Béxar), about `dx: -1.22, dy: 0.10`.
- **Not fixed:** which creek Jack forded (the San Pedro lies between the town and the Alazán, but no source names it);
  the exact ditch; where the Mexican gun stood. **The battlefield has not been located archaeologically in anything read.**

  Rusk's fifteen. **But Yoakum has "the remainder of the army, with the exception of a suitable guard" following**, and
  Taylor has men slipping out "without orders or leave". **Whether the rest came up in time to fire is DISPUTED.** The
  camp guard stayed behind in every version.
- **Share engaged (computed here):** about 140–300 men out of the 405–600 in camp is **about a quarter to three quarters**.

### 6.1 What this means for the game's rule

- **Applying Concepción's rule unchanged overstates the Grass Fight.** At 1.1% killed per fighter, 140 men would be
  expected to lose 1.5 and 300 men 3.3 (computed here). The chance of the real outcome, nobody killed, would be about
  **21% for 140 men and about 4% for 300** (computed here, as 0.989ⁿ). The record points to a lower killed risk.
- **A class's own share.** The measured class sent 19 volunteers (`docs/COLONIES.md` §6h). If most stay into late
  November and a quarter to three quarters of them fight, a class puts about **4 to 14 people** in the Grass Fight
  (computed here). At 3% wounded, that is **0.1 to 0.4 expected wounds, and at 1.1% killed, 0.05 to 0.15 expected
  deaths** (computed here).
- **Bounds the sources would support**, offered for the owner in §14:
  - **killed: zero, or at most about 0.3% a fighter** (the whole-siege rate), still never more than one in a class;
  - **wounded: about 2–3% a fighter**, all slight (Jack: "very slightly"), mending in the game's three days;
  - **missing/ran home: about 0.5–1% a fighter**, the documented man who ran to the settlements.
- **Frailty.** Nothing in the record ties a Grass Fight wound to weakness. The one named injury was a glancing head
  wound, "perhaps by one of his own party" (Taylor). The oldest man there, James Burleson, 60, was praised, not hurt.
  Weighting by hidden strength and health (as Concepción does) is the game's own choice.
- **Horses.** No horse loss is recorded for the Grass Fight. Horses were stolen from camp at night (Nov 16 order), and
  those who stayed had their horses appraised (Nov 24), so a lost horse was expected to be paid for (Austin's report,
  Telegraph Dec 12).

## 7. Women and families

**Negative result: no woman appears in any source read as being in the Texian camps before Béxar between November 2
and December 4, or at the Grass Fight.** The Order Book's orders and the parade of November 24 are all addressed to
men. Bryan's letters describe camp life without mentioning a woman. **This extends `HIST-TEX-023` to November.**

**Where women and families do appear:**

- **Gonzales, November 2–3.** After the army left, Gonzales held "not more than 12 Men", "the balance of the population
  composed of women (whose Husbands are in the Army) and children". The Ayish Bayou volunteers "compelled women to leave
  their House with their Children", broke open doors and robbed (John Fisher, Nov 3, AP III p. 233). L. Smither (Nov 4):
  "almost Evry house in the place broken open and Robd"; "Mrs Dickerson ho had been drivin from her house" asked him to
  guard it, and he was beaten nearly to death there (AP III pp. 236–238). TSHA's Susanna Dickinson entry says she
  "remained in Gonzales through November, when newly arriving troops looted her home". **Reading "Mrs Dickerson" as
  Susanna Dickinson is TSHA's identification; it is STRONGLY SUPPORTED.** Her husband Almeron was a lieutenant of
  artillery at the siege (TSHA). She joined him in San Antonio "probably in December" (TSHA), so she was not at the camp
  in this window. Fisher: "The families of this place unless protected, are determined to risk the Indians" rather than
  such men.
- **At home, working.** Bryan's parents at Peach Point were "picking out Cotton etc." (Bryan, Nov 18). Families sent
  clothing on request (Bryan, Nov 7). Austin wrote of the volunteers that "the most of them are men of families, whose
  loss would have made a fearful void in our thin community" (report of Nov 30, Telegraph Dec 12), a reason he gave for
  not storming.
- **Land.** Borden assured Austin that "no advantages has, or will be taken of those who are in the field" at the land
  office (Nov 5). A family's claim was not lost by a man's absence.
- **Inside Béxar.** Deaf Smith's wife and family were in or near San Antonio (TSHA). Ehrenberg describes women and
  children sheltering in houses during the storming, which is December. **Soldaderas with Cos's garrison: not researched
  here; no source read mentions them for November.**
- **A family that played host.** Bryan's letters were carried by men going to Nacogdoches and by Mr. Jones; letters went
  "To the care of Jno. R Jones San Felipe de Austin who will please forward it" (Nov 7).

## 9. After the Grass Fight, to December 4

- **Nov 27.** Jack writes his report; Burleson writes his to the Provisional Government (Yoakum's citation).
- **Nov 29–30.** Austin reaches San Felipe and reports the army's "patriotism... is unabated"; Bryan writes that there
  is "considerable confusion and dissatisfaction in the Army on account of the acts of the convention" and that "4 or
  500 men" mean to stay "until Bexar falls or they eat up all" (AP III pp. 268–269).
- **Dec 1.** The Provisional Government commissions Burleson commander in chief of the volunteer army (TSHA, Burleson).
  The General Council reads his Grass Fight report and passes its thanks, and enrolls an ordinance "for supplying the
  army of the people now in the field before Bexar, with money" (Council Journal). **Samuel Maverick and John W. Smith are
  released from house arrest in Béxar**, reach the camp and urge an immediate attack (TSHA, Maverick).
- **Early December.** With "limited supplies and approaching winter", Burleson considers withdrawing to Goliad; an attack
  plan is drawn from what the released men report; fear that Cos has learned of it brings "a near breakup of the Texan
  army" (TSHA, Siege; Barr). Ehrenberg describes an assembly at which the commander proposed retiring "behind the
  Guadalupe" until spring. **The exact dates of the withdrawal proposal and the cancelled attack were not fixed in a
  primary source read.**
- **Dec 4.** Returning from a scout, Milam learns the majority means to go into winter quarters and calls "Who will go
  with old Ben Milam into San Antonio?"; **three hundred volunteer** (TSHA, Milam). A Mexican officer who came over
  reported low morale (TSHA, Siege). About 210 answered the roll that night by Ehrenberg's count, a figure no other source
  read gives.
- **Dec 5, before dawn.** Milam and Johnson attack with "more than 300"; Burleson holds the camp with about 400; Neill's
  guns fire on the Alamo as a diversion (TSHA, Siege).

**Where the next step picks up:** on the evening of December 4, one camp at the old mill north of the town, about 700
men by Barr's figures, most of whom had just voted to go into winter quarters, and some 300 of whom have now said they
will go into Béxar at dawn with Milam.

## 11. What the repository has wrong or too simple

Checked against `sim/army.mjs`, `docs/COLONIES.md` §6i and §7a and `HISTORY.md` as of commit `ee0beef`.

1. **After Concepción the army does not move.** The class stops on November 2, so this has not mattered. From here it
   will: the army went above the town (Oct 31–Nov 2), split again with headquarters at Concepción (about Nov 8–15), and
   united at the mill (Nov 15). `MISSIONS` has Espada and Concepción; there is no mill camp. **A siege step needs a camp
   north of the town and a dated move.**
2. **The killed at Concepción are given `siteId: OBJECTIVE`** (`sim/army.mjs`, in `fightConcepcion`) while placed at
   Concepción's coordinates. `OBJECTIVE` is Béxar, which was Cos's town until December. If anything reads `siteId` as
   "where this person lies", it will say Béxar. Worth checking before the siege step reuses the pattern.
3. **The Concepción death rate is not a general battle rate.** `CONCEPCION_DEATH_RISK = 0.011` fits one fight. Reused for
   the Grass Fight it would make a death about as likely as the record makes it unlikely (§6.1).
4. **"The army" as one formation** (`FIC-GONZ-034`, and the Concepción ceiling "the detachment is not drawn apart from
   the main body") is again too simple: on November 26 two parties went out and the camp guard stayed. And unlike
   Concepción, **the Grass Fight parties were drawn across companies**, so assigning by company would be wrong here.
5. **Leaving the army.** `HIST-TEX-022` has the Nov 4 departures. The record adds that from November 10 **no one could
   leave camp without written permission**, that on November 24 the army asked every man to **pledge or not**, and that
   the horses of those who stayed were appraised. "Anybody can be sent for" at any moment remains the game's invention.
6. **Battle news on the day** (`FIC-GONZ-039` ceiling) is further from the record for the Grass Fight than for
   Concepción: five to six days, and the first printed version was wrong on numbers and left out the grass (§8).
7. **`HIST-TEX-023` is dated October**; the same holds in November and should say so, with the Gonzales looting as the
   documented thing that happened to the women left at home.
8. **Army strength.** `armyProjection` shows only the class's members. That is still right. The one count the army took
   itself, 405 on November 24, is a good figure for a milestone text; the rest are estimates.
9. **Wounds mending in three days** fits the Grass Fight's "very slightly wounded". It would not fit the storming, where
   Barr counts 30–35 casualties and the Texas State Library holds a list of wounded.
10. **Two names in the older histories are wrong for the game's text:** Kennedy's "Grass Fight" on November 8, and
    Ugartechea as the leader of the pack train. Neither is in the repository now; keep them out.
11. **The Béxar layout is 4,000 × 3,400 feet** (`docs/BEXAR_ASSEMBLY.md`). The Grass Fight ground, about 6,400 feet west
    of the town point (computed), lies outside it; the mill, 2,600 to 7,900 feet north, may lie at or past its edge.

## 14. Questions for the owner

Each is multiple choice. The research's leaning, where it has one, is marked, but the choice is the owner's.

1. **How far should the class run?**
   - (a) To **November 26**, ending on the Grass Fight.
   - (b) To **December 4**, the eve of the storming, with Milam's call as the last event. *(Section 9 is written for this.)*
   - (c) To **November 24**, Burleson's election, leaving the Grass Fight for the next step.

2. **How deadly is the Grass Fight for a family's volunteer?** (Nobody was killed of about 140–300; 2–4 slightly wounded; one ran home.)
   - (a) **Nobody is killed**; about 3 in 100 are slightly wounded, weighted by hidden strength and health. *(Closest to the record.)*
   - (b) A very small killed risk, **about 0.3 in 100**, never more than one in a class, plus wounds as in (a).
   - (c) The **same as Concepción** (1.1 killed, 2 wounded in 100, at most one death a class).

3. **Who in the class fights on November 26?**
   - (a) **The family is asked** when Deaf Smith rides in whether their person rides out, as on October 22.
   - (b) **Chosen from the seed across companies**, about a third of those in camp, favouring those with a horse; nobody asked.
   - (c) **Everyone in camp that day** fights, following Yoakum's "the remainder of the army".

4. **Should one volunteer be able to run home from the fight, as the missing man did?**
   - (a) **Yes, rarely** (about 1 in 100 fighters); they turn up at home days later, unhurt.
   - (b) **No**; the missing man is only told as part of the history.

5. **Where does the army camp after November 2?**
   - (a) **The real sequence**: above the town Oct 31–Nov 8, split with headquarters at Concepción Nov 8–15, united at the mill from Nov 15. *(Documented.)*
   - (b) **One camp at the mill** from November 2, with the split told in text.
   - (c) **Stay at Concepción** until the Grass Fight.

6. **Leaving and staying in November.**
   - (a) **Automatic neighbours' volunteers go home for winter clothing** in early November at about the documented rate and some come back; **on November 24 every family with someone in camp is asked whether they pledge to stay.**
   - (b) Only the pledge on November 24 is asked; nobody leaves on their own.
   - (c) Neither; leaving stays only by a family sending for someone.

7. **How does a family hear of the Grass Fight?**
   - (a) **By rider, days later, and first as the wrong rumour** ("300 a side, ten dead, no loss"), then the fuller account. *(Documented timing: 5–6 days to San Felipe.)*
   - (b) **On the day**, as Concepción is now, with the Mexican losses as a range.
   - (c) On the day for families with someone in the fight; by rider for everyone else.

8. **Mexican losses (3, 15, 50 or 60 killed by source).**
   - (a) **The range, with its sources**, as at Concepción.
   - (b) **No number**; "they went back into town with their dead and wounded".

9. **The silver.**
   - (a) **Families hear the rumour of a silver train before the fight**, and the grass is the reveal.
   - (b) The grass is told only afterward.

10. **Austin's order to storm on November 21, which fewer than 100 would obey.**
    - (a) **Each family with someone in camp is asked whether their person would go in**, with no consequence beyond the record.
    - (b) Told as history only.
