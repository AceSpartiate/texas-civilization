## The one-line answer

**The army did not walk to Béxar at an even pace. It went stop-and-go: four days to the Cibolo, three days halted
there, the Salado (within five miles of the town) on October 20, six days halted there, then south to Mission
Espada on the 26th–27th. On October 28 about ninety men, four companies under Bowie and Fannin, were attacked at
their camp by the river near Mission Concepción. The main army was about six miles back at Espada and got there
half an hour to an hour after the Mexican troops had gone.** One Texian died: Richard Andrews of Mina, shot in the
body. Nobody else was badly hurt. So only the detachment **fought**; everybody else was **present**. The death rate
the sources support is **1 killed out of 90–92 men engaged (about 1.1%)**, with 0–2 wounded.

## 2. Summary

- **October 13.** The army crossed to the west bank of the Guadalupe and stepped off: "not exceed 300 men" (Order
  Book). It camped that night at "Williams place near Guadalupe".
- **October 14–16.** It went on by "Camp Sandy" (Oct 15–16), where the Gonzales cannon's carriage gave out and the
  gun was left (Smithwick, Creed Taylor). It reached the **Cibolo** on the 16th.
- **October 16–19.** It **halted on the Cibolo** to wait for reinforcements. A council of war voted on the 18th to
  keep waiting and changed its mind on the 19th. Sam Houston visited (Smithwick, Taylor). Bowie joined around here.
- **October 20.** It moved up to the **Salado**, "within less than five miles of Bejar", with Mexican outposts in
  sight on the hill.
- **October 20–26.** It **stayed on the Salado**, skirmishing every day and short of bread and salt. Juan Seguín
  came in on the 22nd. On the 22nd Bowie and Fannin's division went ahead to Mission Espada. On the 24th Austin
  began "the investment". On the 26th some 28–30 Consultation delegates left for San Felipe, and the army moved
  toward the missions with "but four hundred effective men" (Austin).
- **October 27.** Headquarters was at **Mission Espada**. Bowie and Fannin took about ninety men up the river to
  find a camp nearer the town, and camped in a bend of the river near **Mission Concepción**.
- **October 28.** In fog, about half an hour after sunrise, Ugartechea's force surrounded the camp. The fight became
  general about 8 a.m. Three charges were beaten back and a brass fieldpiece was taken. **Texian loss: Richard
  Andrews, mortally wounded; he died that day.** The main army came up 30–60 minutes after the Mexican troops had
  gone. The Mexican loss is disputed, from 14 to 67 killed; see §4.6.
- **October 31 – November 2.** Austin moved above the town to the Alamo canal. Bowie's division moved to 800 yards
  below it. Both councils voted against an assault. The siege settled in.

### 3.1 How fast it actually moved

Taking the repository's 69-mile road, with the Salado camp about 5 miles short of the town:

- **About 64 miles in 8 calendar days (Oct 13 to the morning of Oct 20), about 8 miles a day on average.** This is
  computed here.
- **Halted more than moving**: 3½ days on the Cibolo and 6 days on the Salado. On the days it did march, about
  4½ of them, that works out to roughly **14 miles a marching day**, also computed here. That fits Royall's working
  figure for supply wagons on the road: "You may calculate at 15 miles travel pr day" (AP III p. 178).
- **It never marched to the town.** It went to a creek camp east of Béxar, then *south*, down the San Antonio River
  to Espada, about nine miles below the town (Taylor gives "about ten"). Then it came *north* past San José to
  Concepción, "1½ miles from Bexar" (Order Book heading). Only on Oct 31 did Austin's headquarters sit on the Alamo
  canal "about one mile from town", north of it.

**For the game:** ten miles a day for 69 miles puts the column at the town about October 20. By coincidence that is
the day it really reached the Salado. So the *arrival date* holds. The *shape* does not: an even pace with no halts,
straight into the town, is not what happened.

### 3.2 Who came and went on the road

- **Came:** Bowie with Joseph Ham, Donahue "and several other Louisianians" (Smithwick). Alley's company from Goliad
  by Oct 23. Seguín and his followers, Oct 22. Individual volunteers throughout, since the Order Book on Oct 14
  requires unattached men to enroll. Houston visited but did not stay. The Nacogdoches men were still only expected
  on the 28th. The Oct 26 order sends latecomers along a blazed trail from the Cibolo to Espada.
- **Went:** Hoxey, sent back Oct 19. The Consultation delegates, Oct 26, with perhaps "15 or 20" more who "would go
  back any how" (Bryan). At Goliad, men of Collinsworth's party "have returned home, and others say they will go in a
  few days" (Dimmitt, Oct 21), and on Oct 14 the garrison nearly walked out (B. F. Smith). **After Concepción:**
  "more than 150 men have left this camp to return home for winter clothing and other purposes" (Austin, Nov 4).
  Smithwick "went through the formality of getting a furlough" and went to Bastrop.
- **Rules against it:** on Oct 14, no leaving the ranks without the captain's permission. On Oct 23, at Goliad, a
  man who left his post without permission would be "reported and published as a Deserter". Nobody in the sources
  read was punished for leaving the main army. The Revolutionary Army entry (TSHA) says colonists turned out in a
  crisis and then "dismiss[ed] themselves at apparent lull times or to take off and care for the needs of their farms
  and families."
- **Families sending for men: nothing found.** No letter read shows a family recalling a volunteer. What families
  did do, on the page, was write: James F. Perry to Austin, Oct 27, "tell Austin and Joel to write to us... there
  mother is verry anxious to hear directly from them". **That "anybody can be sent for" is a gameplay rule, not a
  documented practice.** The *outcome*, that a man could leave, is well documented. The *mechanism*, a family calling
  him home, is not.

### 3.3 Supply and sickness

- **Short of food:** bread, Oct 22 ("beginning to suffer greatly"). Salt for less than three days. Bowie and Fannin
  buying beef and corn "with our own funds" (Oct 23). Beeves driven with the army, with men detailed to drive them
  (Oct 13). Corn and beans bought from the Béxar ranchos through Seguín, on credit (Oct 24).
- **Relief:** Goliad's carts, 8 loads on Oct 27–28 and 4 more, including 43 barrels of flour, salt, coffee, sugar,
  liquor, kettles and tobacco (AP III pp. 219–220). "4 waggons have arrived with Supplies" by Oct 28 (Bryan). From
  San Felipe, 300 blankets, 250–300 pairs of shoes and tent cloth were on the road (Royall, Oct 25 and 31).
- **Sickness:** Austin himself, "very bad" from about Oct 17 on ("since I was at Cibolo", Nov 2), and "salivated",
  i.e. dosed with mercury (Bryan, Oct 26). A surgeon was appointed on Oct 18 and captains had to report their sick.
  "we have many sick" (Nov 1). On Nov 4 the effective force was "about 450 men after deducting the sick". **No death
  from sickness in the army is recorded in the sources read for Oct 13 – Nov 4.**

## 4. Concepción, 28 October 1835

### 4.1 The ground

- **Documented (Bowie and Fannin, Telegraph Nov 14):** a bend of the San Antonio River "within about five hundred
  yards of the old mission Conception." The plain in front was "nearly level". The timber formed "two sides of a
  triangle". There was a bluff with a "sudden fall in our rear" (the OCR gives its height as "six to ten feet" or
  close to that) and "a bottom of fifty to one hundred yards to the river." Seven men were posted "in the cupola of
  the mission house, which overlooked the whole country". **The OCR of the bluff height is damaged; read it as
  several feet, not as an exact figure.**
- **Smithwick:** the camp was "on the east side", "a quarter of a mile above the old mission", "some two miles from
  San Antonio", under "the bank, which was five or six feet high", with "the bluff and heavy timber on the west side
  of the river" at their backs, and pecan trees overhead.
- **Taylor:** "a low ground or bottom with high banks skirted with timber in front and the river in our rear"; the
  men called it "The Horseshoe".
- **Austin's heading:** "Mission Concepcion, 1½ miles from Bexar". Bryan: "2 miles from Bexar".
- **Barr (TSHA):** a wooded bend protected by an embankment. Via Wikipedia: Hardin puts the bend 500 yards west of
  the mission with its arms about 100 yards apart; Barr says Concepción was about 6 miles from Espada. Also via
  Wikipedia, a 2023 computer reconstruction reported by the *San Antonio Express-News* puts the site perhaps 2,000
  feet north of the park marker; **the exact ground is not archaeologically fixed.**
- **Fog:** "at dawn of day, every object was obscured by a heavy, dense fog" (Bowie). Taylor says the night was
  clear and fogged over before morning. Smithwick: "The dense fog masked the strength of the enemy."

## 5. Who fought

- **Only the detachment, 90–92 men, fought.** The main army was at Espada: "The main army did not arrive from the
  mission de Espada where it was stationed untill about one hour after the battle" (Bryan). "The main body of the
  army came up in about 30 minutes after the enemy had retired" (Austin). "The main army reached us in about one hour
  after the enemy's retreat" (Bowie and Fannin). "About an hour after it was all over, Austin came up with the main
  body" (Smithwick).
- **The main army's men knew they had missed it.** "the other boys were loud and bitter in their denunciation of the
  course that had deprived them of a share in the glory" (Smithwick). Of four named men with the main body (Joel Bryan, "the two McNeels and Hassell"):
  "neither of them had a chance to distinguish themselves" (Bryan). Taylor: most "were chagrinned at having missed
  the chance to fight".
- **One gap in the arrival story.** TSHA says Austin's men "rushed to the field when they heard firing, but arrived
  too late". Barr, via Wikipedia, says the start from Espada was held up chasing a company that had deserted, and
  that Travis's mounted men rode ahead and saw the Mexican troops still withdrawing. **Neither the deserting company
  nor Travis's early arrival appears in any primary source read.**
- **The seven men in the mission cupola and the pickets were part of the 92.** Smithwick's pickets were cut off at
  the mission and fired from its roof. They were in the fight, not apart from it.

**For the game.** A volunteer marching with the main body on October 28 was **present**, not **fought**. A volunteer
**fought** only if they were in the detachment. The share is about 92 of roughly 400, **about 1 in 4 or 5 (computed
here)**. Which companies went is documented, with the dispute in §4.2. Which of a class's own volunteers stood in
them would be invented (§12). A volunteer who had not caught up by the 28th was on the road behind the army, neither
present nor fought.

**Deaths in the same fortnight that were *not* the army's:** Lt. David M. Collinsworth, killed on Oct 29 near Goliad

### 6.1 What this means for `docs/FAMILY_CREATION.md` §5

- **One death among 92 men engaged is the documented ceiling.** A class that sends, say, 19 volunteers (the measured
  class in §6h), with 1 in 4 or 5 of them in the detachment, puts 4 or 5 people in the fight. **The expected number
  of deaths is about 0.05** (computed here). **One death in a class is already more than the record makes likely.
  Two would break the rule** that no battle kills more of a class's people than its documented losses make
  plausible.
- **The rule "very likely fatal for somebody weak or frail" cannot hold at Concepción** without breaking the
  casualty rule. Nothing in the record ties the one death to weakness. Andrews was by repute the biggest, strongest
  man there (TSHA). Two eyewitnesses say he died by crossing open ground instead of going under the bank (Smithwick,
  Taylor), which is exposure, not frailty.
- **A bound the sources would support**, offered for the owner's decision in §14: per-person death risk in the
  detachment averaging about 1%, never high enough that a class loses more than one person at Concepción; wound risk
  of the same order; zero for the main army.
- **Horses:** "a few horses" were lost (Austin). No number. A family's horse at Concepción can plausibly be lost, and
  this is the one battle so far where the record says horses were.

## 7. Women

**Negative result: no woman appears in any source read as marching with the army from Gonzales, in camp on the Cibolo
or the Salado, or at Concepción.** The Order Book's rules, rolls and details are all addressed to men ("every man in
the army", "evry volunteer"). No source read mentions women cooking, nursing or following the column. **The memoirs
don't either**, though Taylor and Smithwick describe camp life in detail.

**Where women do appear in these sources, they are at home or being moved out of danger:**

- **Gonzales, Nov 3:** after the army left, "not more than 12 Men and 3 or 4 guns were left most of the men were
  invalids, the balance of the population composed of women (whose Husbands are in the Army) and children". East
  Texas volunteers passing through "compelled women to leave their House with their Children" (John Fisher, AP III
  p. 233). This is the clearest period picture of what a family whose man had gone looked like.
- **Goliad and Powers's colony:** "steps have been taken to remove the women and children, beyond the Guardeloupe
  River", and the mission's carts had gone to Victoria "with the women and children" (Dimmitt, Oct 25 and 27).
- **Béxar:** Cos, it was said, threatened to make the leading citizens sweep the plaza and, if he won, to make "their
  Ladies, grind tortias for his soldiers" (Bowie and Fannin, Oct 22). A "respectable gentleman... escaped, with his
  family, from Town" (same).
- **The Permanent Council:** the war's purpose as they wrote it: "to save texas and Protect our Wives and children"
  (Royall, Oct 27).
- **A mother waiting for word:** "there mother is verry anxious to hear directly from them" (Perry, Oct 27).

**What this does and does not support for `docs/MONEY_AND_GLORY.md` §4.** The sources make it **strongly supported**
that in October 1835 the army was men and the women of volunteering families stayed at home, ran it, and were the
ones endangered and moved when the country was threatened. **No source read says, in words, that sending a woman to
fight was held against a family.** The owner's reveal line, *"In 1835, sending a woman to fight was held against a
family"*, is a reasonable reading of a world in which it is never even mentioned. It is not a quoted judgement.
Registering it as **DOCUMENTED** would overstate it. §10 proposes it as **STRONGLY SUPPORTED** context plus a
**FICTIONAL FOR GAMEPLAY** row for the penalty itself, and says what source would be needed to go further.

Not researched here: *soldaderas* with the Mexican army at Béxar. No source read mentions them for October 1835.

## 8. What a family at home would hear

- **The Consultation.** It was called for October 15 and postponed to November 1 "at the insistence of
  delegates-elect and army officers" (TSHA). On October 16, 31 members met at San Felipe, agreed to the delay, and set
  up a General (Permanent) Council to act until then (Telegraph Oct 26; TSHA). Royall, Oct 16: the members "are like
  Volunteers in Camp (Very Restless) and much is said about going home one by one". The members in the army left
  camp on Oct 26 after a vote of the troops (Bryan). **When the quorum came is disputed:** the Telegraph's minutes
  open "Nov. 3, 1835, 2 o'clock, P. M. There being a quorum present", while TSHA says "no actual quorum existed
  until the fourth." B. T. Archer was chosen president. On November 7 it voted 33–14 for a provisional government
  "upon the principles of the Constitution of 1824" (TSHA).
- **Goliad.** The garrison nearly broke up on Oct 14, and B. F. Smith resigned the command and was reappointed. On
  Oct 15 Dimmitt succeeded to the command. On Oct 21 it had "still 50" effective men, and many of the first party had
  gone home. Karankawa raids on stock on the Navidad and Guadalupe were stopped by agreement (Oct 27). Supply carts
  went to the army (Oct 27–28). Collinsworth was killed on the road on Oct 29. Twenty men came from Bay Prairie on
  Oct 30. A detachment was ordered against Lipantitlán (Oct 31) (AP III pp. 182–223).
- **News reaching the settlements.** The Telegraph of **Oct 26** had the army "on the Salado creek, about five miles
  from Bejar" and volunteers "continually flocking", and said "Hundreds have left their plantations" at cotton-picking
  time. The **Oct 31** issue, set in type after the rest, carried "VICTORY! VICTORY!": an action "on Wednesday morning
  last", ninety men "under the command of captain Fanning" against four hundred, "about fifty" of the enemy lost and
  "one man wounded" of ours. **So news of Concepción reached San Felipe within three days, about 150 road miles**,
  and it arrived *wrong in detail*: Bowie not named, Andrews reported wounded, not dead. **Nov 7** printed Austin's
  report and the Consultation's thanks. **Nov 14** printed Bowie and Fannin's official account.
- **From outside.** 65 volunteers from New Orleans on the schooner *Columbus*, with two guns (Telegraph Oct 31). The
  New Orleans Greys at Brazoria about Oct 27 (E. Andrews). An 18-pounder and a 12-pounder started up the country
  (McKinney and Williams, Oct 26; Royall, Oct 29).
- **Rumours** a family would have heard and should not take as fact: 500 Mexican troops about to reach Béxar;
  Savariego raising the Irish of San Patricio against Goliad (Austin calls it only a report, Nov 1–2); 300 troops
  marching from Matamoros (Austin: "by no means to be credited").

## 9. After the battle, to November 2

- **Oct 28, afternoon.** Austin wanted to push straight on to the town, and the officers were against it (TSHA; Taylor
  as "camp gossip"). The army camped at Concepción. Bryan expected 100 Nacogdoches men the next day.
- **Oct 31.** Austin's headquarters moved "on the Alamo Canal at the mouth of a dry gully about one mile from town",
  north of it. Travis went with 50 men after a herd of 900 horses sent toward Laredo. Men of two presidial cavalry
  companies offered to desert (AP III p. 225). Taylor dates the move to the old mill on the last day of October.
- **Nov 1.** Bowie's division moved to "about 800 yards below Town", brought its baggage up from Concepción, and
  bridged the river. Austin demanded surrender, and Cos sent back that he would hold the place "if he had only ten men
  left". Four cannon shots were fired at Austin's line and hurt nobody. "we have many sick."
- **Nov 2.** Two councils of war voted **not to storm**: at headquarters every officer but Maj. B. W. Smith, and in
  Bowie's division all but two. Bowie's officers voted to join the main army above the town, and Austin ordered it.
  Burleson occupied the mill.
- **Nov 3–4.** Austin asked the Consultation for a regular army and battering cannon, and reported "more than 150
  men have left this camp to return home for winter clothing".

**Where the next step picks up:** a divided army closing in on a fortified town from above (north) and below (south),
with no assault; men drifting home and East Texans arriving. The Grass Fight (Nov 26) and the storming (Dec 5–9) come
after, as in `HIST-TEX-007`.

## 11. What the repository has wrong or too simple

Checked against `sim/army.mjs`, `sim/directors.mjs`, `HISTORY.md` and `docs/COLONIES.md` as of commit `00793a9`.

1. **The army never goes to the town.** `OBJECTIVE = 'bexar'` and `advanceArmy` sets `phase = 'arrived'` with
   `siteId = OBJECTIVE`, which puts the army in Béxar. The army stopped at the Salado, about 5 miles east (Oct 20),
   went down to Espada, about 9 miles south (Oct 26–27), and fought at Concepción, 1½–2 miles south (Oct 28). It
   got closest to the town on Oct 31, a mile north. Béxar was Cos's, and the army did not enter it until December.
   **Arriving at the town site is historically wrong** and will be visible once the Béxar layout is drawn.
2. **The pace is too smooth, but the date is right.** `ARMY_MILES_PER_DAY = 10`, with its comment "which is when it
   was there", now has a documented check: the Salado on Oct 20. The number happens to give the right arrival. What
   it hides is two halts (Cibolo, Oct 16–19; Salado, Oct 20–26) totalling nine or more days. A family watching its
   volunteer's dot would see steady progress that did not happen. The comment's "what is documented is when they
   left, not how fast they walked" is now out of date: the Order Book dates every camp.
3. **The army is one body; the battle happened because it wasn't.** `FIC-GONZ-034` makes the army a single formation.
   From Oct 22 it was two: Bowie and Fannin's division ahead at Espada, then at Concepción. **`fought` versus
   `present` can't be decided without a detachment.** `recordPresent` gives every member the same role; step 6 needs
   a way to split members.
4. **"Anybody can be sent for" is looser than the record in one way and fine in another.** Leaving was common and
   unpunished (`HIST-TEX-022`), so letting a volunteer go is sound. But the documented reasons were delegates going to
   the Consultation, men going home for winter clothes after Concepción, and furloughs. No family recall was found.
   The army also had a rule against leaving the ranks without a captain's permission. The control is a fair
   invention. It should stay registered as one, and it might cost a little time, a furlough, rather than being
   instant.
5. **`HIST-TEX-013` and `docs/COLONIES.md` §1 say Briscoe's Liberty Volunteers "fought at Concepción".** That rests on
   TSHA. The commanders' own report names Fannin's, Coleman's, Goheen's and Bennet's companies, not Briscoe's. Briscoe
   was in Bowie's division by Nov 2. **Treat it as DISPUTED** until a roll or Barr's source is found. This matters
   because the owner is from Liberty and the game already routes a Liberty volunteer to Briscoe's company.
6. **`docs/COLONIES.md` §3a and §5.5 list Goliad among the battles where `fought` applies.** Goliad was taken on the
   night of Oct 9–10 by Collinsworth's party, *before* the army was made (Oct 11) or marched (Oct 13) (`HIST-TEX-018`).
   A volunteer who went to the Gonzales rendezvous could not have been there. Only somebody who joined Collinsworth
   on the coast could. §3a's "Kerr's letter reports one Mexican soldier killed and one Texian wounded" also conflicts
   with `HIST-TEX-018`, which excludes Goliad's casualties as disputed.
7. **`docs/COLONIES.md` §5.5 milestones say "the march for Béxar (October 11–12)"**, and the comment on
   `advanceGathering` in `sim/directors.mjs` says the army "marches on the twelfth". The built game and `HIST-TEX-018`
   use the 13th. Stale text, not a behaviour bug.
8. **`docs/FAMILY_CREATION.md` §5, "very likely fatal for somebody weak or frail", cannot be applied at Concepción**
   without breaking §5.5's casualty rule (§6.1 above). The one man killed was reputedly the strongest. §5's list of
   where death can happen ("Béxar, the Alamo, Goliad, San Jacinto") does not name Concepción, though §5.5 does. It
   needs one answer.
9. **No army strength is asserted, and that is right.** `armyProjection` shows `strength: army.members.length`. The
   documented strengths (300, 400, 450) are estimates written by Austin, and the game should keep showing only what
   the class sent, perhaps set against "about four hundred" in a milestone text.
10. **The slice ends on October 17** with "what happened at Concepción... is still ahead". On the 17th the real army
    was halted on the Cibolo, not "on the road". That is fine as an ending line, but a stop at the halt would be truer
    than a stop mid-road.

## 12. What would have to be invented

- **Which of a class's volunteers are in the detachment.** The share, about 92 of about 400, is documented. The
  choice is not. Choices open to the owner: by company (a settlement's volunteers go with their settlement's
  company, e.g. Mina's with Coleman's, which *is* documented); at random; or by the family's own choice of "go ahead
  with Bowie".
- **Each person's outcome**, from hidden stats, inside the bound in §6.1.
- **The companies' home settlements** beyond Coleman's (Mina) and Briscoe's (Liberty, disputed). Fannin's, Goheen's
  and Bennet's are unknown here.
- **The exact ground**: the bend, the bank height ("five or six feet" by Smithwick; the report's figure damaged in
  OCR), where the mission stands relative to it. The site is not archaeologically fixed.
- **The timing inside the morning**: when the fog lifted, how long the long-range firing ran. Only "half an hour by
  sun" and "about eight o'clock" are given.
- **The Mexican losses on screen.** No count can be asserted. Show the field, the carts taking the dead away, and no
  number.
- **What the class hears first.** The documented Oct 31 report got the wounded wrong and left out Bowie. Using that
  is invented only in who carries it.
- **A family's horse lost**: documented as "a few horses", with no number and no owners.
- **A woman in the detachment**: no documented case, so any is invented, and the owner's penalty applies.
