## The one-line answer

**After Béxar fell, the colonists went home.** Burleson resigned on December 15 "and returned to his home. Many of the
men did likewise." By the end of December about 400 men were left under Frank Johnson, most of them volunteers newly
come from the United States. On December 30 Johnson and James Grant took most of them, and "almost all of the supplies",
south toward Goliad for an attack on Matamoros, leaving James C. Neill at Béxar with fewer than 100 men, "not a dollar",
no horses to speak of and no clothing. The provisional government at San Felipe tore itself apart over that expedition:
on January 10 the council impeached Governor Smith, and by late February it had two members left. Houston, commander of
an army that barely existed, talked most of Johnson's men out of Matamoros at Refugio on January 21 and then went on
furlough to treat with the Cherokees. Bowie reached Béxar on January 19 with 30 men and decided to hold it; Travis came
on February 3 with 30 cavalry, Crockett on February 8. **On February 1 the settlements elected delegates to a convention
at Washington-on-the-Brazos, called for March 1** — Gonzales chose Mathew Caldwell and John Fisher. Meanwhile Santa
Anna's army of about 6,000 marched north through a winter that dropped 15–16 inches of snow on it on February 13,
crossed the Rio Grande about February 12–16, and was on the Medina, 25 miles from Béxar, on February 21. Warnings
reached Béxar from about February 16 and were argued over rather than believed. **For most families in the colonies
this was a winter at home: the men back from Béxar, pay and land promised but not given, rumours of Santa Anna coming
in the spring, an election, and the corn to plant.**

## 2. The army goes home, December 1835

- **December 15:** "Burleson resigned his leadership of the army on December 15 and returned to his home. Many of the men
  did likewise." (Wikipedia, "Texas Revolution".) This matches `bexar-end` in the game (`sim/directors.mjs`).
- **Late December:** about 400 men remained under Frank W. Johnson, elected commander of "remaining forces, primarily U.S.
  volunteers" (TSHA, Matamoros Expedition). "Of the volunteers serving from January through March 1836, 78 percent had
  arrived from the United States after October 2, 1835." (Wikipedia, citing Lack.)
- **So the colonist volunteers the game follows were, with few exceptions, home by Christmas.** The men in the Alamo in
  February were mostly not the men who had taken Béxar — with the conspicuous exception of the Gonzales company that
  rode in on March 1 (the next research document).

## 3. What a man could sign up for

Houston's proclamation, Washington, **12 December 1835** (Texas State Library, primary):

| Service | Terms |
| --- | --- |
| Regular army, two years or the war | "$24 and 800 acres of land" |
| Auxiliary volunteers, the war | "640 acres of land" |
| Volunteers, one year | "320 acres" |
| Shorter service | no land; "liberal pay, rations, etc." |

He warned that Santa Anna "has ordered to Texas ten thousand men", and asked for five thousand volunteers by March 1.
The Council "followed the instructions of the Consultation and kept the land offices closed" (TSHA, General Council):
**the land was a promise, not a title, all winter.** "Few of the volunteers agreed to join Houston's regular army"
(Wikipedia).

## 6. Santa Anna's march, and when anyone knew

- **Army:** 6,019 soldiers had begun marching by December 1835 (Wikipedia). Supply failed: "not enough mules", teamsters
  quit unpaid.
- **The snow:** "By February 13 an estimated 15–16 inches of snow had fallen"; recruits from the tropics died of
  exposure and dysentery (Wikipedia).
- **The Rio Grande:** Wikipedia's Alamo article has the crossing February 12; another Wikipedia-derived summary has Santa
  Anna at Guerrero February 16 and Urrea at Matamoros February 17. **Disagreement noted; the game needs only "mid-February".**
- **The Medina:** February 21, 25 miles from Béxar; a surprise attack that night was called off because rain made the
  Medina unfordable (Wikipedia, Siege of the Alamo).
- **Warnings at Béxar:** Ambrosio Rodríguez warned Travis on February 16 and 18; Blas Herrera's report that the vanguard
  had crossed came on the 18th and a council of war "argued over whether to believe the rumors" that night (Wikipedia).
  A tejanovolunteercompany.com summary dates Herrera's sighting to February 10 and his report to February 11; TSHA says
  "about the middle of February". **Disagreement noted.**
- **February 20–22:** many Béxar residents packed and left; on the 22nd most of the garrison attended a fandango for
  Washington's birthday (Wikipedia).
- **Further east:** flight began in the far south "as early as January 14, 1836, when the Mexicans were reported gathering
  on the Rio Grande" — San Patricio, Refugio, San Antonio (TSHA, Runaway Scrape). The Tornel decree of late December
  declared foreigners taken in arms "pirates" (Wikipedia).

## 7. What families did and heard

What the sources above establish for an ordinary colonist family between Christmas and February 22:

1. **The men came home** from Béxar, most by late December. A few stayed with Johnson, Neill or later Fannin.
2. **There was no pay and no land yet**: promises of 320–800 acres for enlisting, with the land offices closed.
3. **The government in San Felipe fell apart in public**, over Matamoros, and families heard it as quarrel and rumour.
4. **February 1: an election** for delegates to a convention on March 1. In Gonzales, Caldwell and Fisher were chosen.
5. **Rumour of Santa Anna** coming in the spring grew through January and February; in the far south people were leaving
   from mid-January.
6. **Work at home.** Not established by the sources read today, and therefore **not asserted**: that corn was planted in
   February–March, hogs killed in the December cold, fences and houses mended. These are the ordinary colonial farm
   calendar, but they need a source before they become a `HIST` row. Proposed as `FIC-GONZ-044` until then.

## 10. What the repository has wrong or too simple

- **The class ends at `bexar-end` with the ending**: glory revealed and the winner named (`sim/ending.mjs`). With two
  class periods, day 1 cannot reveal the winner of a war that is not over.
- **Wounds and absences are measured in days of the 1835 calendar**: a severe Béxar wound of 21 days or a dangerous one
  of 60 runs across the gap between the periods. What happens to them over the gap is a decision.
- **A family's stores are eaten per day**: the gap from December 15 to January is weeks; what a family eats over it is a
  decision, and a harsh answer could starve a family that did nothing wrong.
- **The Host has "New Class"**, which archives a class. A second period needs **"Continue to the next period"** instead:
  the same save, the same family keys, the same people.
