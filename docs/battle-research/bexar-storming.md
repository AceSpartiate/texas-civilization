## The one-line answer

**On the afternoon of December 4 the army before Béxar was loading its wagons to go into winter quarters. A Mexican
officer deserted into camp and said the town could be taken. Ben Milam called for volunteers, and somewhere between
210 and 300 men said they would go in. Before dawn on December 5, while Neill's single gun fired on the Alamo as a
feint, they went into the town in two divisions. Milam's division took the de la Garza house and Johnson's took the
Veramendi house, both on Soledad Street north of the Main Plaza. For four days and nights they fought from house to
house toward the plaza. They broke walls with crowbars, cut loopholes, dug trenches across the streets and filled
sandbags. About 100 more men were sent in from the reserve of about 400–500 that Burleson kept at the mill camp.
Milam was shot in the head in a house yard at about half past three on December 7, and Johnson took command. On the
evening of December 8 Ugartechea reached Cos with about 600 men, most of them untrained conscripts. That night Cooke's
Greys and Patton's men broke into the Priest's House on the plaza. In the small hours of December 9 Cos pulled back
into the Alamo, several cavalry companies rode away, and at dawn a white flag came out. The terms, agreed about 2 a.m.
on December 10 and dated December 11, sent Cos and his officers south of the Rio Grande on parole not to oppose the
Constitution of 1824. Burleson's army then went home.** Texian dead were **4 to 6** (Milam and at least three others,
two of whom died of wounds). Wounded were **14 to about 35**. The surgeon's own list names **23**: 2 mortal, 7
dangerous, 11 severe and 3 slight. Taking about 300 men as the assault force, the rates are **about 1.7% killed** and
**about 7–8% wounded, most of them seriously** (computed here). **The reserve recorded no loss.** Mexican losses are
reported as anything from about 150 killed and wounded (Barr) to "seven hundred and forty dead" (Ehrenberg).

## 2. Summary

- **December 1–3.** Maverick and J. W. Smith come out of Béxar (Maverick: left town Dec 1, reached camp Dec 2) and urge
  an attack. A plan to take the Veramendi, de la Garza and Cárdenas houses under Morris, Somervell and Jack fails on
  the night of Dec 3–4, when Somervell and Jack "were not ready" (Maverick), or when Burleson countermands it on a
  false report that the plan had been betrayed (Cooke 1844; Stiff; Creed Taylor). A council of war on the night of Dec
  3 votes to raise the siege (Johnson 1880s).
- **December 4.** At parade, orders to march for winter quarters (Gonzales per Johnson 1880s; La Bahía per Maverick,
  Dance, Stiff, Cooke 1844 and Field). Men leave in squads: "250 or 300 set off for home" (Maverick). In the afternoon a
  Mexican lieutenant deserts in. Milam calls for volunteers. They elect Milam to lead and organise in two divisions at
  the old mill after dark.
- **December 5.** At about 5 a.m., or earlier, Neill fires on the Alamo. The divisions go in down Acequia and Soledad
  streets and take the de la Garza house (Milam) and the Veramendi house (Johnson). They are pinned there all day.
  Colonel Grant and Lt. Ward are badly hurt (Ward loses a leg). Trenches are dug that night.
- **December 6.** The Mexicans man the rooftops. Lt. McDonald's detachment of Crane's company takes a house ahead and
  to the right. Sandbags are filled that night.
- **December 7.** About noon Henry Karnes breaks a door with a crowbar and York's company takes a house in front of
  the first division. **About 3:30 p.m. Milam is shot in the head as he passes into the yard of Johnson's position.**
  At 7 p.m. the officers make Johnson commander with Morris second. At 10 p.m. four companies take the Navarro house.
  The night is "exceedingly cold and wet".
- **December 8.** At 9 a.m. the same companies with Greys take Zambrano Row, room by room. At 7 p.m. four more
  companies go into the Row. **In the evening Ugartechea arrives with reinforcements.** A party of about fifty from the
  Alamo fires on the Texian camp and is driven off. At 10:30 p.m. Cooke's Greys and Patton's company break into the
  Priest's House on the plaza, and the heaviest cannonade of the siege follows.
- **December 9.** Before dawn Cos concentrates in the Alamo, and several cavalry companies ride away south. At about
  6:30–7 a.m. a white flag comes to the plaza. Commissioners are named. Burleson comes in about 9 a.m.
- **December 10–11.** Terms are agreed about 2 a.m. on Dec 10 (Burleson 14 Dec). The document is dated and signed Dec
  11. The army is paraded on Dec 11 and votes on it company by company, "a small majority for it" (Dance). There is a
  fandango.
- **December 13–15.** The stores are inventoried (Dec 13). **On Dec 14 Cos marches out to Mission San José**, and
  Burleson writes that "the rest of the army will retire to their homes". The General Council hears of the victory on
  Dec 15.

### 3.2 The deserter and the call

- **The deserter.** Every account has a Mexican officer who came over on the afternoon or near sunset of December 4 and
  said the town was weak. His name is **DISPUTED**: "Al Feris [Alveris?], Cornet of horse" with a white flag (Maverick);
  "Lieutenant Vauvis", captured by the scout Bates Berry (Creed Taylor); unnamed "Mexican lieutenant" with five riders
  and a white flag (Ehrenberg); a lieutenant (Cooke 1844; Johnson 1880s; Dance). TSHA: "a Mexican officer surrendered
  with news of declining Mexican morale". Maverick adds that "another deserter had just come in with dispatches from
  Cos to Ugartechea".
- **Who made the call.** "Who will go with old Ben Milam into San Antonio?" is in TSHA (Milam), Johnson 1880s, Creed
  Taylor and the 1849 *Gazette*. **Who started it is DISPUTED.** Johnson (1880s) says he suggested it to Milam. Cooke
  (1844) says he raised about 300 by marching his Greys "up and down the lines", then "proposed the name of Benjamin R.
  Milam as the leader" and Milam "was elected unanimously". W. T. Austin (1844) says Burleson "authorized" Milam and
  Johnson to raise volunteers. Creed Taylor has Milam draw a line on the ground with his rifle stock (the only source
  with a line, and it looks borrowed from the Alamo story; **not to be used**). Maverick: "by the animating manner and
  untiring zeal of Col. Milam... the men fall into ranks to see if we are strong enough."
- **Could men decline? Yes, openly.** Everyone read agrees the assault force was volunteers. Creed Taylor has Burleson
  ask those "unwilling to join in the assaulting column" to "remain in camp and be ready to assist". Ehrenberg: a
  signing list got **230** names, but at the 2 a.m. roll "only two hundred and ten men" answered. Dance: "300
  volunteers", then "200 of us", "100 of our men having backed out". Nobody read was punished for staying out.

### 3.5 Burleson's reserve, and Neill's diversion

- **The reserve.** Burleson "formed all the reserve, with the exception of the guard necessary to protect the camp, at
  the old mill position", and held it ready (Burleson 14 Dec; W. T. Austin letter). **Size:** "another 400" (TSHA);
  "about five hundred strong besides the attacking divisions" (W. T. Austin); "not twenty men" in camp when the
  Mexicans sortied (Lopez, not credible). **About 400–500 is STRONGLY SUPPORTED.**
- **What the reserve did.** Mounted companies under Cheshire, Coleman and Roberts scoured the country "to intercept
  Ugartechea" and brought in provisions (Burleson 14 Dec; W. T. Austin). The quartermaster sent barbecued beef into
  town (Creed Taylor). **Reinforcements went in:** "Captains Cheshire, Sutherland and Lewis, with their companies"
  (Burleson 14 Dec), "about one hundred men" (W. T. Austin letter); "150 men from camp and other parts of the country"
  (Dance); "Burleson sent 100 men into town" on December 8 (TSHA). Splane's, Ruth's and Borden's companies, with
  Somervell and Sublett, were "kept in readiness" and did not go in (Burleson 14 Dec). On the evening of the 8th
  "about fifty men" from the Alamo fired on the camp and were driven off by a six-pounder under "Capt. Hummings"
  (Burleson 14 Dec; "Capt Parrot" per Dance) and Bradley's company. W. T. Austin says cavalry came on the west bank and
  infantry on the east, and "declined making the intended attack". **No loss in the reserve is recorded.**
- **Neill.** "At three o'clock on the morning of the 5th instant, Col. Neill, with a piece of artillery, protected by
  Capt. Roberts and his company, was sent across the river to attack, at five o'clock, the Alamo, on the north side",
  and "the party returned to camp at nine o'clock a.m." (Burleson 14 Dec, DOCUMENTED). "About five oclock in the

  east Side" (Stiff); "For two hours Niell's gun played upon the fortress" (Creed Taylor). Dance thought it "did not
  have the desired affect". The *Gazette* (1849) has Neill joining Milam in town afterwards. TSHA (Neill) says "from
  December 5 to 10 Neill, in command of a single gun and a small contingent, provided a successful diversion".
  Burleson's own report says the party came back by 9 a.m. on the 5th. **DISPUTED** whether the diversion went on after
  the first morning.

### 3.6 Where they went in

- **Streets.** Two columns "along Acequia (now Main) and Soledad Streets" (marker). Milam's "down Acequia Street",
  Johnson's "entering the town at the bead [head] of Soledad Street" (Creed Taylor, who wrongly makes the Navarro house
  Milam's first objective). Leandro Chavez: "the columns that had entered by Acequia Street". Field (Johnson's
  division): "down the river through a street... to within fifty yards of the square. Here Johnson's division broke
  into a house upon the left, or river side of the street, at the same time that Milam took others on the right."
  Ehrenberg (Breece's column) went "down the road along the river" and first sheltered in "an old guard-house".
- **The approach.** They crossed "the cornfield" from the mill (Ehrenberg), dropped blankets and coats about 200 yards
  out (Stiff; Lopez), crossed a brush fence, and were fired on by one or two sentinels. Deaf Smith shot one (Stiff; Cooke
  1844; Lopez, who says the sentinel hit Smith in the wrist). Fog (Creed Taylor); a norther and cold (Ehrenberg).
  Rockets went up over the Alamo (Ehrenberg). **Time:** "at day light or rather some twenty minutes before"
  (Milam–Burleson); "about day break" (Cooke 1844); "at three O'clock we hurried noiselessly" (Ehrenberg); "about two
  hours before day" (Field).
- **The Veramendi house.** Johnson's division (Johnson; Cooke 1844), "Bowies house" (Stiff; Bowie had married
  Veramendi's daughter). Its HABS record gives **130 Soledad Street** with its "west elevation (front)", so it stood on
  the **east, river side** of Soledad Street. That matches Field's "left, or river side". It was demolished in 1910
  (search results for the HABS record). **Distance from the plaza is not given in any source read**, beyond Field's
  "within fifty yards of the square" for the point where the columns stopped.
- **The de la Garza house.** Milam's division (Johnson; Cooke 1844). The marker: a compound that "occupied an entire
  city block" with limestone walls "three feet thick". It says "Milam's troops engaged in a two-day battle to
  commandeer" it, **which conflicts with Johnson and Cooke, who have it taken on the first morning.** It was demolished
  in 1912 with "a sixteen-pound cannonball... embedded in the walls". The marker stands at the northeast corner of
  Soledad and East Houston streets, "on the right when traveling south on Soledad", i.e. on the west side.

### 4.1 How the house-to-house fighting was done

- **Holding stone houses.** Flat-roofed stone and adobe houses with a parapet "about four feet above the surface of
  the roof" (Creed Taylor), walls "3 or 4 feet thick" (Dance). Men on the roofs were driven down, so the fighting was
  done through **loopholes cut in the walls**: "in less than one hour made the houses look like a pigeon nursery"
  (Lopez). **Documented in Johnson** as "port-holes" only by implication; explicit in Dance, Field, Ehrenberg, Lopez and
  Creed Taylor.
- **Getting between houses: trenches across the streets.** "Opening trenches, and effecting a safe communication"
  (Johnson, Dec 5 and 6, **DOCUMENTED**). "We all set to work to dig a trench between their building and ours" and cut
  doors "in the thick walls of the houses at each end of the passage" (Ehrenberg). "Cut ditches so that we could get to
  water" (Stiff). Tools were short: "the want of proper tools" (Johnson).
- **Getting into houses: crowbars, axes and walls broken through.** Karnes's crowbar (Johnson, **DOCUMENTED**). "With
  crowbars we perforated the walls of the houses toward the square" (Field). "Axes crobars mat axes & cutting through
  walls of houses 3 or 4 feet thick" (Dance). The Mississippians broke in a wall in ten minutes (Ehrenberg). Doors were
  beaten down and "the occupants [driven] from one apartment to another" (Field; Johnson, Zambrano Row, "from room to
  room").
- **Through the roofs.** "The Texians sought their enemies by breaking through the flat roofs of the houses, and letting
  themselves down by their blankets" (Field). One man lowered in a blanket fell "into the middle of a fire" (*Gazette*
  1849). The Mexicans tried the same against Zambrano Row (Dance).
- **Sandbags and breastworks.** "Making and filling sand bags" (Johnson, Dec 6, **DOCUMENTED**). A breastwork "in the St.
  with Bags of dirt" (Dance). Cooke's men in the Priest's House had "lost our sand bags" and dug the floor with Bowie
  knives (Cooke 1844; Stiff).
- **Burning cover.** Wooden houses and fences between the lines "were set on fire by us" (Field); the mesquite burned
  (Ehrenberg).
- **The Mexican defences.** At "the entrence of each street was a dich 9 feet deep and 15 wide and imbankment throwed on
  sides a Breast work and mounted cannon to rake every St" (Dance). "At the entrance of every street, with the exception
  of that leading to the Alamo, a ditch was dug ten feet wide, five feet deep, raised on the inner side, so as to make an
  elevation of ten feet. Over this was erected a breast-work of perpendicular posts, with port-holes for muskets, and
  one in the centre for cannon" (Field). **Archaeology in 2007** found a slope up from the river and "a trench in which
  they would have placed wooden posts to form a palisade" under Main Plaza (marker 30214). Maverick: timbers taken to
  the Alamo and the streets blockaded from Oct 13 to 17; 5 guns in the town (4 on the plaza, 1 in front of the church)
  and 7 at the Alamo, with an 18-pounder on the old church, by Oct 26. **The ditch dimensions are DISPUTED** (9 × 15 ft by
  Dance; 5 × 10 ft by Field). The palisade is **STRONGLY SUPPORTED** by the archaeology.
- **Why the guns did little harm.** The guns were laid to sweep the middle of the streets, so men keeping to the sides
  were rarely hit (Creed Taylor; Lopez). Mexican musketry went high (Ehrenberg; Creed Taylor). Texian rifle fire kept
  the gunners off their pieces (Johnson; Ehrenberg). **Reading, not a source:** most Texian hits came from roofs, open
  crossings and yards.
- **Food and water.** Two days' rations each, and "by morning of the second day there was not a morsel" (Creed Taylor);
  barbecued beef sent in from the camp. Water came from the river under fire (Ehrenberg; Stiff; Dance's two buckets of
  milk).
- **Flags.** A "crimson flag" (Ehrenberg) or "black flag" (Dance) over the Mexican battery or church. The Texian
  commissioner "Tompson" tells Sánchez Navarro "yesterday you flew a black flag on the battery" (Sánchez Navarro).
  **STRONGLY SUPPORTED** (three sources, two sides).
- **No order, only volunteers.** "No orders were given. Whenever special duties had to be performed, the call was sent
  out for men ready to offer their services" (Ehrenberg). Johnson names companies sent. **DISPUTED** how far this went.

### 4.3 The Mexican side: reinforcements, collapse and retreat

- **Cos's force before Ugartechea: about 570** (TSHA/Barr). Ehrenberg's and the *Gazette*'s 1,200–1,500 are not
  credible.
- **Ugartechea.** He arrived on the evening of December 8 (Johnson; Burleson "11 Dec"). TSHA: "over 600 reinforcements,
  but only 170 were experienced soldiers. Untrained conscripts formed the other 450 men, who brought with them few
  supplies." The capitulation speaks of "the one hundred infantry lately arrived with the convicts" and "the convicts
  brought by Colonel Ugartechea". Cos: "tired from a twenty three league march", lacking training, and "increasing the
  consumption of provisions". Sánchez Navarro, who came with them: "80 replacements... did not know even how to load".
  Smith's circular (Dec 9) expected "six hundred"; Creed Taylor and Ehrenberg say 500. **About 600, mostly untrained
  conscripts, is STRONGLY SUPPORTED.**
- **The collapse, night of December 8–9.** Cos decided to concentrate in the Alamo and began moving artillery and
  supplies (Cos; Sánchez Navarro). Sánchez Navarro held the plaza guns by the cemetery "30 varas" from the Texians with
  Condelle, "about 70 men". About 1 a.m. the cavalry was ordered to saddle. At 4 a.m. came a rumour "that the Comandante
  General is dead and 4 capitanes have fled with their companias": Elguézabal, Ignacio Rodríguez, Juan José Galán
  (Agua Verde) and Manuel Barragán (Río Grande), with "more than 400" said to follow. Cos himself: "two companies of the
  Presidiales de Rio Grande with their officers, plus those at Agua Verde and some pikemen... had deserted me", and "only
  120 of the Permanent Battalion, Morelos, and some Presidial Dragoons remained". **How many rode off is DISPUTED:**
  "short 174 men" (Creed Taylor); "possibly 175 soldiers from four of the cavalry companies" (Todish via Wikipedia);
  "more than 400" (rumour); "four companies of his cavalry" (TSHA). Sánchez Navarro later said they misunderstood orders
  (Todish via Wikipedia).
- **At the Alamo.** "About 50 horses that were eating the capes of the troops and even the trails of the artillery...
  girls and women who had taken refuge there... no supplies at all not even water" (Sánchez Navarro). Cos: "Sanchez, by
  reason of cowardice and perfidy of many of our companions all is lost... Go save those brave men."
- **The plaza abandoned.** Condelle withdrew his men and refused to surrender because "el Batallon Morelos has never
  surrendered" (Sánchez Navarro). "About Sun Rise Dec 10th their fireing sudently ceased it was foggy... they had left
  behind Some Dead and wounded and Retreated across the River" (Dance, **wrong day**).

### 5.2 What the capitulation said

From the signed English text (**DOCUMENTED**), eighteen articles:

1. **Cos and his officers "retire with their arms and private property into the interior of the republic under parole
   of honor; and that they will not in any way oppose the reestablishment of the federal constitution of 1824."**
2. The hundred infantry who came with the convicts, "the remnant of the battalion of Morelos, and the cavalry", retire
   with Cos, **"taking their arms, and ten rounds of cartridges for their muskets."**
3. **Cos is to take the convicts brought by Ugartechea "beyond the Rio Grande."**
4. **The troops may choose "to follow their General, remain, or go to such point as they may deem proper"**, keeping
   their arms.
5. All public property, money, arms and munitions are inventoried and delivered to Burleson.
6. Private property is restored to its owners.
7. and 8. Three officers of each army make and deliver the inventory.
9. **Cos occupies the Alamo "for the present" and Burleson the town, and "the soldiers of neither party pass to the
   other, armed."**
10. **Cos removes his force "within six days".**
11. Cos may take "a 4-pounder and ten rounds of powder and ball."
12. The inventory begins at once.
13. **"The citizens shall be protected in their persons and property."**
14. **Burleson furnishes provisions to the Rio Grande "at the ordinary price of the country."**
15. **Cos's sick and wounded, with a surgeon and attendants, may remain.**
16. **No one, citizen or soldier, is to be "molested on account of his political opinions hitherto expressed."**
17. Copies in Castilian and English.
18. **Prisoners of both armies are freed.**

**What the capitulation does *not* say.** It does not make the men prisoners of war, and it does not require Cos
himself to cross the Rio Grande. It sends him "into the interior" on parole, and only the convicts "beyond the Rio
Grande". Dance's summary ("surender as prisoners of war give up their commisions take an oath never again to take up
arms against... the federal constitution of 1824") and Ehrenberg's ("to cross the Rio Grande into Mexico as soon as
possible"; "a hundred and fifty rifles") **overstate it**. TSHA (Cos): released "on their pledge not to oppose further the
Constitution of 1824". **The parole was against opposing the Constitution of 1824, not against fighting Texas.** Texians
later read Cos's return in 1836 as breaking it (TSHA Cos).

**What was surrendered** (Inventory, Dec 13): at Béxar a four-pounder, 30 useless muskets, drums, 66 hats and 49
blankets of the lancers. At the Alamo about a dozen pieces from three-pounders to a nine-inch culverin and a five-inch
howitzer, and 257 carbines and muskets. In the arsenal about 11,000 musket cartridges, grape, powder, flints. From
Manchaca's store 117 muskets, 166 bayonets, 71 lances and duck jackets. Barr (via Wikipedia) totals "400 small arms, 20
cannon". Neill was put "in charge of more than thirty captured field pieces" (TSHA Neill). Ehrenberg's "forty-eight
cannon... four thousand muskets" is not credible.

**How many marched out with Cos:** "one thousand one hundred and five having left this morning with Gen. Cos, besides

## 7. Who fought

- **The share.** About 210–300 went in at first, about 100 more were sent in later, and about 400–500 stayed in the
  reserve (§3). **So roughly 35–50% of the army that stayed after December 4 fought in the town at some point**
  (computed here from 310–400 of about 700–800). At first light on December 5 it was **about 30–40%** (computed).
- **It was volunteer, not by company.** Each man chose (§3.2). Whole companies are named in Johnson, but they were the
  companies that volunteered, and Ehrenberg says his was missing only its wounded. Creed Taylor has men who "refused to
  go into the assault" loafing in camp until the quartermaster put them to work.
- **Colonists versus United States volunteers.** "Those that Remained and took Sanantonio were generaly men that owned
  no land in the Country" (Dance). The citizens dispersed on Dec 4, "except among the Volunteers from the United States,
  who having no homes in the country were in a measure forced to remain" (Cooke 1844). Ehrenberg: the Greys and the
  Mississippians carried the attack. **But** Johnson's divisions include clearly colonial companies (York's, Patton's,
  Swisher's, Alley's, Benavides's) and Seguín's Béxar men, and Barr (via Wikipedia) says losses were "spread evenly
  amongst Texas residents and newcomers". **DISPUTED**: US volunteers were over-represented, but colonists went in too.
- **Tejanos.** Plácido Benavides's company was in Johnson's division (Johnson). Seguín's company, "on December 5...
  participated in the assault" (TSHA Seguín). Leandro Chavez of Béxar, a carter with no enrolment, crossed under fire
  "from Verramendi's to Antonio Garza's house" and was wounded (Chavez petition; Seguín and Bustillo affidavits).
  Julián Díaz of Espada "entered Bexar with the Texian troops" in Seguín's company (Díaz petition; Seguín and Menchaca).
- **Men who changed their minds.** "Many of the men who had left for home... changed their minds on hearing of our
  intended attack" and rejoined Burleson (Cooke 1844). Dance's 100 "backed out", and Ehrenberg's 20 missed the roll.
- **For the game.** On the evening of December 4 a volunteer still in camp could say yes to Milam and go in (fought),
  stay with the reserve (present), or already be on the road home. Being sent in later with Cheshire's, Sutherland's or
  Lewis's reinforcement is a documented fourth path. **The record supports asking the family**, because the choice was
  personal and public. **It supports no settlement exclusion**, since colonial companies from Brazoria, Washington,
  Victoria and Austin's colony went in.

### 8.2 What this would mean for the game's rules

- **This is the deadliest fight so far, per fighter, but only for those who went in.** About 1.7% killed is
  Concepción's rate plus about half as much again. The wound rate is three to four times Concepción's, and the wounds
  were serious.
- **Expected losses for a class.** The measured class sent 19 volunteers (`docs/COLONIES.md` §6h). If about 8–12 are
  still in camp on December 4 (**the game knows its own figure; this is an illustration**) and a third to a half go in,
  that is **3–6 fighters**. At 1.7% killed and 7–9% wounded that gives **0.05–0.10 expected deaths and 0.2–0.5
  expected wounds**. The chance of two deaths among 6 fighters is under half of one percent (computed as 15 × 0.017²).
  **"Never more than one killed in a class" still fits the record.**
- **A bound the sources would support**, for the owner in §15:
  - **killed about 1.5–2% per fighter**, weighted by hidden strength and health as at Concepción, never more than one
    in a class (and whether deaths from wounds count against that cap is a question, §15);
  - **wounded about 7–9% per fighter**, graded roughly as the surgeon did: **about 1 in 8 slight, half severe, a third
    dangerous**, with a small share of the dangerous later dying;
  - **zero for the reserve**, including Neill's party and the camp guard.
- **Frailty.** Nothing ties these deaths to weakness either. Milam was 47 and died from exposure in a yard. The worst
  wounds came from standing on roofs, crossing streets and working guns (Johnson; Ehrenberg: "almost all the gunners
  had been severely wounded"). **Reading, not a source:** exposure, not constitution, decided who was hit. Weighting by
  hidden stats remains the game's own choice.

- **Where the wounded were kept.** In Béxar, not sent home. Twenty-nine severe cases were "put into the hospital", and
  the lightly hurt "found lodgings in the city" (Ehrenberg), under Doctors Levy and Pollard (Johnson). Cos's wounded
  stayed too, with their own surgeon (Capitulation art. 15).
- **What the government promised.** The ordinance of December 5 gave volunteers enlisting "for and during the war" 640
  acres, and a man "disabled in the service... discharged therefor" the same (*Telegraph* Dec 12 p. 2). Land descended
  to the heirs of a volunteer who died. **That applies to men enrolled under that ordinance.** Whether it reached the
  volunteers of 1835 is not settled here. Barr (via Wikipedia) says participants were later granted 320 acres, with 504
  claims certified.
- **Reading for the game (not a source):** *slight* = days, as now. *Severe* = weeks, kept at Béxar, and some men were
  back in service within three weeks to three months. *Dangerous* = months, with a real chance of a lasting mark (a
  lost leg, eye or arm) and a small chance of death afterwards. **No source gives a healing time in days for any grade.**
  The durations are estimates from a handful of careers.

## 9. Civilians and women; what families heard, and when

### 9.1 The people of Béxar inside the town

- **Families shut in their houses.** "The soldiers on forcibly entering a house... found a number of females, who had
  been shut up for three days without sustenance" (Field). In the Mississippians' breach: "Terrified screams of women
  and children inside told us that the house was full of helpless people"; "a train of women, children, and men
  staggered" out and were let go "back to the houses which lay between our present quarters and the camp" (Ehrenberg).
  One old man running across the street was riddled (Dance). **STRONGLY SUPPORTED** (three Texian accounts).
- **The woman at the river.** Each of three accounts has a Mexican woman fetching water for the Texians who is shot, and
  **they disagree on what happened to her**: "four bullets went through her body and she fell lifeless" (Ehrenberg); "was
  shot through the arm... and did not reach our Station" (Stiff); she "received a shot from a whole platoon within the
  Alamo, which inflicted five separate wounds... returned with her water, and was doing well" (Field). Ehrenberg says she
  had been kept "to cook our food and bake our bread". **That a Béxar woman was shot carrying water is STRONGLY
  SUPPORTED. Whether she lived is DISPUTED.**
- **The priest's family.** In the Priest's House, "the priest, 1 son and 3 Daughters the son attempted to make his
  escape through a window but was shot by one of the Grays We kept the old priest and 3 daughters until the surrender"
  (Stiff). Sánchez Navarro names the parish priest, "don Refugio de la Garza", whose house "I almost leveled last
  night", and who stayed with the Mexican commissioners. **Stiff alone has the family and the shooting** (DISPUTED).
  That the priest's house was taken with the priest in it is supported by both sides.
- **Families fled to the Alamo and to the Texian camp.** "Girls and women who had taken refuge there leaving their
  houses abandoned and open in Bexar" (Sánchez Navarro, at the Alamo, Dec 9). "The Mexican familys that had fled to our
  camp for protection were in a distressing condition" (Dance, Dec 4).
- **Food from the townspeople.** "Turtiers a bread made of boild corn ground on a stone by the weman" (Dance, after the
  surrender). Austin had recommended paying Béxar's people for the corn and beef the army took (*Telegraph* Dec 12 p. 3).
  Article 13 of the capitulation protected citizens' persons and property.
- **Soldaderas and the garrison's dependents.** **Not found.** No source read uses the word or describes camp women
  with Cos's troops in December. The women at the Alamo (Sánchez Navarro) are described as townswomen who had left
  their houses. Ugartechea's family had come from Monterrey on October 8 (Maverick). **Gap.**
- **Anglo residents.** Maverick, John W. Smith and A. C. Holmes had been held at Smith's house until December 1; "the
  women" of Smith's household were there on November 11 (Maverick). Deaf Smith's wife and family lived in or near
  Béxar (grass-fight.md §7). Susanna Dickinson joined her husband Almeron in San Antonio "probably in December" (TSHA,
  grass-fight.md). **When is not fixed**, so she cannot be placed in the town during the fighting.

## 10. What happened next, to about December 20

- **Cos goes.** On December 14 "General Cos left this morning for the mission of San José, and, to-morrow, commences his
  march to the Rio Grande" (Burleson 14 Dec). Cos wrote from "Ranch Salinas, fifteen leagues from" Béxar, heading for
  Laredo, stopping a day "to acquire the necessary carriages to transport my wounded" (Cos to Tornel). Creed Taylor's
  "four days later" fits. **DOCUMENTED.**
- **The army breaks up.** "To-morrow I leave the garrison and town under command of Colonel Johnson, with a sufficient
  number of men and officers... The rest of the army will retire to their homes" (Burleson 14 Dec). Burleson resigned
  on December 15 and went home (Barr via Wikipedia). "Most of the Texas volunteers went home after the battle" (TSHA).
  "The Citizen soldiers were disbanded, and returned to their homes — The Volunteers from the United States consented to
  remain in charge of the town" (Cooke 1844). Creed Taylor took "a thirty day furlough" home to the Guadalupe below
  Cuero, bringing a horse, silver-mounted saddle, sword, blankets, spurs and pistols as trophies. Díaz and "the most part
  of the volunteers" of Seguín's company got leave "to retire and protect their families very much exposed to the
  frequent attacks of Indians" (Díaz; Seguín and Menchaca). **DOCUMENTED** that most colonists went home in mid-December.
- **The garrison: Johnson, then Grant; Neill later.** On December 17 Johnson listed the officers "of the Volunteer Army
  of Texas" at Béxar: **Johnson commander in chief, Grant second**, Morris already commissioned, Neill lieutenant colonel
  of artillery with Captains Almeron Dickinson and T. K. Pearson, Cooke's and Breece's Greys, Llewellyn's company, Burk's
  Mobile Greys, and John Cameron over "Native Troops". The Council commissioned Neill lieutenant colonel of artillery on
  December 7 (TSHA Neill). **The garrison was left under Neill only after Johnson left for San Felipe in late December
  and Grant marched about 300 men toward Goliad on January 1, 1836, leaving Neill "fewer than 100 men"** (Cooke 1844;
  TSHA Neill; Maverick, "DEC. 31ST... & JAN. 1ST... Men set out for La Bahia"). **So "the garrison left under Neill" is
  true of January, not of December 20.** New companies arrived: the Mobile Greys, Lawrence's mounted Tennesseans and
  Pearson's artillery (Cooke 1844). 120 Alabamians and Tennesseans were at Nacogdoches on December 12 (*Telegraph*).
- **Tejanos.** Seguín's company (37 men raised in the fall, TSHA) and Benavides's took part in the assault (§7). Salvador
  Flores had led the Tejanos who opened the Grass Fight ("the Sacate Fight", Díaz; Seguín and Menchaca) and was in
  Seguín's company. A Mexican Federalist, Col. José María González, came to the camp by December 10 with Mexican troops
  and appealed to Cos's soldiers to change sides (González proclamation, Dec 10; Robinson to González, Dec 3). Governor
  Smith told Burleson to arrest him if he "should seem not to act in good faith" (Smith to Burleson, Dec 9). Grant and
  González "brokered the defection of most of Cos's forces" (TSHA Grant; **DISPUTED**, since the Mexican sources blame
  cowardice and confusion). **What Seguín's and Flores's men did between December 11 and 20 was not found**, beyond leave
  to go home.
- **The Béxar volunteers.** Townsmen who guided or fought: John W. Smith, Maverick, Deaf Smith, Hendrick Arnold,
  Leandro Chavez, Julián Díaz, Antonio Menchaca (affidavit). Article 4 let Cos's own presidial soldiers stay, and "three
  companies and several small parties... separated from him" (Burleson 14 Dec). Some local men stayed in the town.
  Whether any joined the Texians in December was not found.
- **Where the next step would pick up.** About December 15–20: Cos on the road to Laredo; Burleson's colonists riding
  home in squads with trophies, and the badly wounded lying in Béxar; Johnson and Grant with the United States
  volunteers holding the town and the Alamo and talking of Matamoros; the Council calling for thirty-day volunteers who
  are no longer needed; and families hearing first a rumour and then the real news.

## 12. What the repository has wrong or too simple

Checked against `sim/army.mjs`, `sim/directors.mjs`, `HISTORY.md`, `docs/COLONIES.md` §6i/§6k/§7a/§7b,
`docs/BEXAR_ASSEMBLY.md` and `public/bexar-layout.js` at commit `23bc700`.

1. **`HIST-TEX-035` gives the assault force as "more than 300" and "about 300 answered".** The sources give about 210 to
   300. The one count from camp records is 216, and the official next-day figure is "about 300". The final milestone
   in `sim/directors.mjs` ("about three hundred answered") is defensible but should become a range.
2. **`HIST-TEX-035` says Burleson "considered withdrawing to Goliad".** The council of December 3 voted to go into
   winter quarters, at Gonzales per Johnson (1880s) and at La Bahía per five other sources. The destination is
   disputed. The row also leaves out the **deserter**, who is in every account as what turned the army.
3. **`HIST-TEX-035` has Maverick and Smith released on December 1 and urging attack.** Maverick left town on the 1st and
   came into camp on the 2nd. Creed Taylor has them escape at 3 a.m. on the 4th. Minor, and disputed.
4. **The mill camp is placed a mile north** (`SIEGE_CAMPS.mill`, `dy: -1`; `FIC-GONZ-040`). Field puts it "within
   one-half a mile" and W. T. Austin "six hundred yards above the town", with the battery 300 yards from the square.
   `SIEGE_CAMPS.above` and `.mill` are also the same point. **About 0.35–0.5 mile is better supported for December.**
5. **Wounds.** The game has only `minor-injury` (three days) and `dead`. The storming's documented wounds were mostly
   severe or dangerous, including an amputation, a lost eye and a lost arm (§8.3). **Three days is wrong for about 18
   of 23 listed wounds.** No state exists for "kept at Béxar under the surgeon" or for a lasting disability.
6. **Battle news on the day** (`FIC-GONZ-039` ceiling) is now further from the record: an express took about 2½ days and
   was wrong, and the victory took 5–8 days and arrived garbled (§9.3). The Grass Fight step already moved to riders.
7. **The army is one body** (`FIC-GONZ-034`, the ceiling in §6k). The storming needs at least three states: in the town,
   in the reserve, and gone home on December 4. It also needs a reinforcement moving from one to another on December 8.
   `recordPresent` and `siteId: OBJECTIVE` would need the same care as at Concepción (grass-fight.md §11 item 2). For
   the first time, the army really is in Béxar.
8. **The death rule.** `CONCEPCION_DEATH_RISK = 0.011` understates the storming slightly for those who went in (about
   0.017) and overstates it badly for the reserve (0). `GRASS_WOUND_RISK = 0.03` is less than half the storming's wound
   rate.
9. **The Béxar assembly is illustrative.** Its Alamo sits about 0.27 mile east and 0.28 north of the town anchor; the
   real Alamo is about 0.45 east and 0.12 north of the Main Plaza (§3.7). It has no named Veramendi house, Garza house,
   Navarro house, Zambrano Row or Priest's House. `docs/BEXAR_ASSEMBLY.md` already says street fighting needs separately
   researched and versioned geometry. Any battle layer must not treat its plots as the 1835 streets.
10. **The repository's Béxar point (GNIS) is effectively the Main Plaza** (0.02 E, 0.02 S of the marker, §3.7). That is
    convenient, and correct for "the town". The army's objective houses lie about 0.1–0.15 mile north of it.
11. **`MISSIONS.concepcion`** (`dx: 0.06, dy: 2.32`) differs by about 0.1 mile east from Wikipedia's coordinate for the
    mission (+0.15, +2.28). Minor; noted only.
12. **The brief's own premise:** "the garrison left under Neill" is true of January 1836, not of December 20. Johnson
    commanded at Béxar in mid-December with Grant second (§10).

## 13. What would have to be invented

- **Who among a class's volunteers goes in**, and with which division. Documented: the choice was personal and
  public, and about a third to a half of those still in camp went. No roll exists.
- **Who goes home on December 4.** The totals are documented (a large part of the army, "250 or 300"). The people are
  not.
- **Each person's fate** inside §8.2's bounds, and **how long each grade of wound takes**. No source gives a healing
  time.
- **Whether a reserve volunteer is sent in** with the reinforcement of December 8.
- **The ground.** The Veramendi house's exact place, the Navarro house of 1835, Zambrano Row, the Priest's House, the
  street trenches and the Mexican ditches are not fixed. Only the Main Plaza, the de la Garza marker and the Alamo have
  coordinates.
- **The hour-by-hour shape** outside Johnson's stated times (7 a.m. Dec 5; noon, 3:30, 7 and 10 p.m. Dec 7; 9 a.m., 7
  and 10:30 p.m. Dec 8; 6:30 a.m. Dec 9).
- **Mexican losses on screen**: a range, not a number.
- **What happened to civilians** the class might see, beyond the documented episodes, and anything about soldaderas.
- **Weather by day** beyond Johnson's "cold and wet" (Dec 7–8), Ehrenberg's norther (Dec 4–5) and Dance's fog (Dec 9 or
  10).
- **Which rumour reaches which settlement when**, outside San Felipe (Dec 8, 12, 15) and Quintana (Dec 17).
- **Trophies**: Creed Taylor's horse, saddle, sword and pistols are one man's; the game's share of spoils would be
  invented.

## 15. Questions for the owner

Each is multiple choice. Where the research leans one way, that option is marked. The choice is the owner's.

1. **How far should the class run?**
   - (a) To **December 9**, the white flag.
   - (b) To **December 14–15**, Cos marching out and Burleson's army going home. *(Leaning: it closes the arc, carries
     the terms, and lets the news reach San Felipe on the 15th.)*
   - (c) To about **December 20**, with men arriving home and the coast rumour.

2. **Who goes in with Milam?**
   - (a) **Each volunteer still in camp on December 4 is asked on their own card**, as at Concepción and the Grass
     Fight. Families nobody plays say yes about a third to a half of the time. *(Leaning: the record shows a free,
     personal choice.)*
   - (b) Chosen from the seed, about a third of those in camp, nobody asked.
   - (c) Everyone still in camp goes in.

3. **December 4 itself: can a family's volunteer go home that day, as "250 or 300" did?**
   - (a) **Families nobody plays send some home at about the documented rate**; a played family is asked whether its
     volunteer goes into winter quarters or stays, before Milam's call. *(Leaning.)*
   - (b) Only families nobody plays lose volunteers; played families are not asked.
   - (c) Nobody leaves on December 4.

4. **How deadly is the storming for those who go in?** (4–6 killed and about 23–35 wounded of about 210–400.)
   - (a) **About 1.7 in 100 killed and about 8 in 100 wounded**, weighted by hidden strength and health, never more than
     one killed in a class; **nobody in the reserve is hurt**. *(Leaning.)*
   - (b) The same as Concepción (1.1 killed, 2 wounded in 100), for those who go in.
   - (c) The whole army's rates (about 0.7 killed and 4 wounded in 100) for everybody in camp, reserve included.

5. **What does a serious wound do?** (Levy's list: 3 slight, 11 severe, 7 dangerous, 2 mortal.)
   - (a) **Three grades**: slight mends in three days as now; severe keeps the person in Béxar under the surgeon for
     about three weeks; dangerous for about two months, with some chance of a lasting mark (a lost limb or eye) shown
     on their card. *(Leaning; the durations are estimates, §8.3.)*
   - (b) **One serious grade**: about three weeks at Béxar, no lasting marks.
   - (c) Keep **three days** for every wound.

6. **Can a wounded person die of the wound afterwards, as Cook, Alexander and Peacock did?**
   - (a) **Yes, rarely, and it counts against the one-death cap** for the class. *(Leaning: keeps the cap honest.)*
   - (b) Yes, rarely, outside the cap.
   - (c) No; only deaths in the fight.

7. **Reserve volunteers and the reinforcement of December 8** (Cheshire's, Sutherland's and Lewis's companies, about
   100 men).
   - (a) **Families with a volunteer in the reserve are asked on December 8 whether they go in**; a yes makes them
     fighters from then on. *(Leaning.)*
   - (b) Chosen from the seed, about one in five of the reserve.
   - (c) The reserve stays out entirely.

8. **How does a family hear of the storming?**
   - (a) **By rider, as the record has it**: on December 8–9 the wrong first report ("on the 6th", "possessed of the
     town"), then about December 15 the victory with Milam's death, and on the coast a garbled rumour ("fled with 100
     men"); each family's own person's part comes with the fuller word, and a badly wounded person's family hears that
     they are lying at Béxar. *(Leaning; it matches the Grass Fight step.)*
   - (b) On the day, as Concepción does now.
   - (c) On the day for families with someone in the town, by rider for everyone else.

9. **Mexican losses** (about 150 per Barr, about 300 per Dance and W. T. Austin, 740 dead per Ehrenberg).
   - (a) **"About 150 to 300 killed and wounded", with the sources**; Ehrenberg's figure left out. *(Leaning.)*
   - (b) All four figures, as at the Grass Fight.
   - (c) No number.

10. **The surrender date** (Johnson, Dec 9; Maverick, Dance, Stiff and Ehrenberg, Dec 10; the document, Dec 11).
    - (a) **White flag on December 9, terms at 2 a.m. on December 10, the capitulation dated December 11**, with the
      disagreement recorded. *(Leaning.)*
    - (b) Everything on December 10.

11. **Civilians in Béxar.**
    - (a) **Told in text**: families shut in houses, townspeople let go through a breach, a woman shot carrying water
      (whether she lived is disputed), women fleeing into the Alamo; nothing shown wounded. *(Leaning.)*
    - (b) Shown as figures leaving houses, with the woman at the river told in text.
    - (c) Left out.

12. **The capitulation's terms in the game's words.**
    - (a) **As signed**: parole not to oppose the Constitution of 1824, muskets and ten rounds kept, the convicts beyond
      the Rio Grande, soldiers free to stay; and a line that Texians later called Cos's return in 1836 a broken parole.
      *(Leaning.)*
    - (b) As the memoirs remember it: prisoners of war sworn never to fight Texas again.

13. **Glory for going in.**
    - (a) **Fought (weight 3) for those who went in, present (2) for the reserve, nothing for those who went home on
      December 4.** *(Leaning; the same weights as before.)*
    - (b) As (a), plus an extra award for the reinforcement that went in on December 8.
    - (c) Going home on December 4 costs glory, as running from the Grass Fight does.
