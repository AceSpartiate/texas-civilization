# The Mexican advance through the Runaway Scrape, 6 March – 30 April 1836, and the retreat after San Jacinto

Research for the Runaway Scrape's armies. **Built the same day** (docs/SCRAPE.md, `sim/advance.mjs`): §9's rows are registered in
`HISTORY.md` as `HIST-TEX-580` to `-599`, and what the game invents as `FIC-GONZ-460` to `-469` (§10, as registered). Read with
[goliad-scrape-san-jacinto.md](goliad-scrape-san-jacinto.md), which covers the Texian side and the battles.

Researched 2026-09-26. Most dates are from **the Mexican commanders' own accounts in Castañeda's 1928 translation**. That
text was read directly: the book's full text on archive.org was searched for lines and read in sections. Other pages went
through a fetching tool that returns a summary with quoted sentences, like the earlier research. Each Mexican general
wrote to blame the others, so trust each one on **his own column's dates** and much less on the others'. Check wording
against the pages before quoting any of it to a class.

Labels: **DOCUMENTED** (a contemporary or eyewitness record, or two good sources agreeing), **STRONGLY SUPPORTED** (one good
secondary source, or a primary one that is self-serving), **DISPUTED** (sources disagree; both given), **TRADITION** (told
later without a record behind it).

## The one-line answer

**Four Mexican columns went east after the Alamo, and none of them moved as fast or as close together as the game has them.**
**Ramírez y Sesma** left Béxar on March 11 and found Gonzales still smoking on the 14th. By about March 20 he was on the
Colorado with about 725–800 men, two miles from Houston at Beeson's. **Tolsa**'s brigade of about 600 joined him in the last
week of March. They began crossing at the Atascosito crossing on March 27, the day after Houston left.
**Santa Anna** did not leave Béxar until March 31, with his staff and thirty dragoons. He was at Gonzales on April 2, the
Colorado on April 4–5 and the burnt San Felipe early on April 7. He took Thompson's ferry above Richmond by a ruse on April
12 and left on the afternoon of the 14th with about 700 foot, 50 horse and one six-pounder. He burned Stafford's plantation
on the 15th and entered Harrisburg that night (Filisola says the 16th). Harrisburg burned, but by whose hand and on which
day is disputed. He went on to New Washington, which he burned when he left on the morning of April 20, and lost at San
Jacinto on the 21st, after Cos had brought him 400–540 more that morning.
**Gaona** left Béxar on March 24 with 725 men for Nacogdoches by way of Bastrop. He was turned toward San Felipe, looted
Bastrop and lost his way. He reached the Brazos only about April 19–20.
**Urrea** fought at Refugio on March 14–15 and at Coleto on March 19–20, and took Victoria at 7:30 a.m. on the 21st. Then
**Texana on April 1, a flooded Colorado on April 6–11, Matagorda at 10 a.m. on April 13, Mrs. Powell's on the 20th,
Columbia on the 21st and Brazoria at 10 a.m. on the 22nd**. He was recalled on the 23rd and never reached Velasco.
**The retreat** gathered at Mrs. Powell's on April 24–25, some 2,563 soldiers and about 1,500 camp followers. The rear
guard burned her house on the 26th. The army waded "un mar de lodo" (Filisola), a sea of mud, between the San Bernard and
the West Bernard from April 26 to 29. It reached the Atascosito crossing on the evening of April 29, crossed from May 1,
and was at Victoria by May 10–13.

**On burning.** Most towns that burned were burned **by the Texians**: Gonzales, San Felipe, Goliad, the houses at Refugio,
and Beeson's and Thompson's on the Colorado road. **The Mexicans burned Harrisburg, New Washington, Stafford's plantation and
Mrs. Powell's**, and a secretary's note says "several houses along our route". Victoria, Matagorda, Columbia and Brazoria
were **occupied and plundered of stores, but no record found says they were burned.** For farms, the record is general:
foragers took corn, cattle and hogs, houses were stripped for lumber and searched, and one family came home to a floor
"torn up by the Mexicans in searching for eggs" (Dilue Rose Harris). No source gives a distance that foragers worked from
the line of march.

**Cos's march.** Filisola was ordered on the 17th to send Cos with 200 men toward Velasco. On the 18th he was ordered

**On farms, honestly:**
- **DOCUMENTED, but only in general.** Santa Anna authorised Urrea to feed his troops on the colonists' cattle and all their other
  property (Urrea, citing an order of Mar 23). Filisola says the Texians' own plan was
  to lay waste the country ahead of the army. Caro sums it up with all the fields laid waste, all the towns burned
  by one side or the other, and the cattle scattered in the woods.
- **DOCUMENTED at named places:** corn and beans taken from the San Bernard houses (Apr 20); flour, beans, sugar and lard
  from Cox's Point (Apr 1–4); lumber for barges hauled from outlying houses near San Felipe (Apr 8) and at Atascosito (Apr 30);
  corn, sheep and hogs at Stafford's (Apr 15); Morgan's warehouses at New Washington; the warehouse goods at Matagorda,
  Columbia and Brazoria.
- **STRONGLY SUPPORTED (an eyewitness, written later):** the Rose farm near Stafford's Point. The floor was torn up by soldiers
  looking for eggs, everything left behind was wrecked, and hogs were loose in the house on May 1.
  Dewees and the Cummins Creek Germans found their houses destroyed in May, the agent unknown.
- **Not found:** any record that the Mexican columns set out to burn farms as a policy. The burnings that can be named are
  of towns, of a few plantations and of houses on the line of march. **The game's rule that every farm is burned is a choice the
  owner made, not a finding** (`FIC-GONZ-046`); the record supports *stripped and looted* far more widely than *burned*.

**The refugees' roads.** From the Brazos below San Felipe and from Harrisburg the road east ran by Vince's bridge to
**Lynch's ferry** over the San Jacinto (`lynchburg`). Dilue Rose Harris counted about five thousand people there, the Brazoria
and Columbia planters and their slaves among them, and waited **three days** to cross. From there it went to the
**Trinity at `liberty`**, where the ferry was swamped, the bottom flooded and sick children died. The Harrises stayed at
Liberty three weeks. Others went on toward the **Sabine**, some as far as it before word of San Jacinto caught them in May.
From the upper settlements the road ran north and east by Washington to `nacogdoches` and the Sabine; the coast families
went by boat to Galveston Island. The Harrises were home on the night of April 30 – May 1 by way of Lynchburg, the
battlefield (Apr 26) and a roundabout road, because Vince's bridge was burned. All DOCUMENTED (Dilue Rose Harris; TSHA).

## 7. The burn zone, as map segments

The country the columns actually crossed, as segments between map places. **"Range" is miles either side of the line**
that foragers and small parties reached. **No source gives a forager range.** The documented detachments are:

- Santa Anna's search for a crossing, two leagues (≈5 mi) up and down the river at San Felipe.
- Lumber for boats "from distant houses".
- Almonte's 50 dragoons, 16 miles from Harrisburg to New Washington.
- Urrea's eight dragoons, about 20 miles from Texana to Cox's Point.
- The corn party left in the San Bernard houses.
- Tolsa's orders to sweep Bolivar, West Bay, Chocolate Bayou and Goose Creek, which were never carried out.
